# Directed nonreachability as a Boolean-corner limit on bipartite graphs

The support of a positive-scaled limit of no-edge probabilities on polynomial-size bipartite graphs can express every language in NL/poly if convergence is required at Boolean inputs. The construction uses a single input literal per nonconstant physical parameter. It also gives an explicit linear-size, subcubic family whose corner limit has a support requiring exponentially many CNF clauses. Its normalized probability diverges at the cube midpoint.

## An augmenting path with an input-independent baseline

Fix a directed graph with distinct source \(s\) and sink \(z\). Each arc is enabled by a literal of the input or a Boolean constant. Discard arcs entering \(s\) or leaving \(z\). For each internal state \(v\) put an always-active left-right pair \(v_Lv_R\) into a bipartite graph. Include unmatched vertices \(s_L,z_R\). For an arc \(u\to v\) guarded by \(\ell\), insert a three-edge path \(u_L-a_R-b_L-v_R\), with \(a_R,b_L\) active exactly when \(\ell=1\). Include a separate isolated edge whose endpoints are active precisely when \(\ell=0\). All four vertices are private.

On a Boolean input, retain just the active vertices. There is a baseline matching of size \(B=(\#\text{internal states})+(\#\text{arcs})\). Use every state's default edge, and use either the center edge or the isolated edge for each arc. Only \(s_L\) and \(z_R\) remain unmatched. An augmenting path for this matching traverses successive oriented arcs of the original graph, switching from each arriving \(v_R\) to \(v_L\) along its default edge. Conversely, any simple directed source-to-sink path augments the matching. By the bipartite matching-cover theorem, the minimum vertex cover thus has size \(B+1\) if the sink is reachable and \(B\) otherwise.

Give each physical vertex probability \((1-t)\ell\), where \(\ell\) is its activation condition and \(0<t\le1\). For a fixed Boolean input, the inactive vertices are absent, whereas every active vertex is absent with probability \(t\). Therefore the leading power of the no-edge probability is the minimum cover size of the active graph. Its positive leading coefficient counts minimum covers. Multiplication by \(t^{-B}\) gives a finite Boolean-corner limit, positive precisely for directed nonreachability.

The length-specific configuration graph of an advised nondeterministic logspace machine has polynomial size. Transitions at that length use constants or one bit-test literal; the advice is fixed. The complement is also in NL/poly, by applying logspace complementation to the machine on an input/advice pair. Taking the complement machine's configuration graph realizes the support of the original language. Add a fresh degree-one source and sink with constant-true connecting arcs. Split in- and out-branching of all previous states with directed binary trees of constant-true arcs. This keeps every internal state at indegree and outdegree at most two while preserving reachability. The resulting bipartite graph is subcubic. [Immerman, *Nondeterministic Space Is Closed under Complementation*](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/abs/10.1137/0217058)

## An explicit separation

Use \(k\ge1\) stages with parallel arcs labeled \(x_i,y_i\) from stage \(i-1\) to stage \(i\). The resulting \(O(k)\)-vertex simple bipartite subcubic graph has \(B=3k-1\). Its corner-limit support is the function
\[
 D(x,y)=\bigvee_{i=1}^k(\neg x_i\land\neg y_i).
\]
A CNF deciding \(D\) needs at least \(2^k\) clauses. The \(2^k\) inputs selecting exactly one variable of each pair are false, and the intersection of any two distinct such inputs is true. Each clause's false inputs form a subcube closed under bitwise intersections.

For comparison, any positive-scaled affine subdivision or other bipartite graph representation whose limits stay finite also at the cube midpoint has a CNF of \(O(N^2)\) clauses, where \(N\) counts the graph vertices. Here is the argument. For a cover \(C\), the event that all its vertices are absent has probability \(\prod_{v\in C}q_v(x)\), with \(q_v=1-p_v\). Their union is the no-edge event. At the midpoint \(z\), normalizing each \(q_v\) by its midpoint value bounds the result by two on the cube. If that midpoint value is zero the affine form vanishes identically. Along a subsequence of a fixed graph type, all these normalized affine forms have limits, with positivity given by a single disjunction of literals (or a Boolean constant). The weights \(c\prod_{v\in C}q_v(z)\) of all covers have finite limits because they are bounded by the normalized event at \(z\). An input has positive limiting probability exactly when one cover with positive limiting midpoint weight uses only vertices with positive normalized factors there.

The good covers form a lattice under union on the left and intersection on the right, or the reverse. The two resulting covers have a weight product equal to the product for the original covers, hence both have positive limiting midpoint weight. A family of omitted-vertex demands is feasible iff each individual and each opposite-side pair of them can be omitted in a good cover. Indeed, for each right demand, combine one witness per left demand, taking intersection on the left and union on the right. This meets all left demands while preserving the fixed right omission. Combine the resulting covers by taking union on the left and intersection on the right. One-sided sets of demands use the corresponding single operation. Infeasible demands thus supply at most \(N+N^2\) clauses, each a disjunction of the positive-factor predicates on one or two vertices. If there are no good covers, the support is empty. This proves the midpoint comparison, including positive scales that diverge.

Hence a midpoint-bounded bipartite representation of \(D\) requires \(\Omega(2^{k/2})\) vertices. In the explicit linear-size corner family, midpoint divergence is direct. At that point all conditional endpoints have an absence probability between \(1/2\) and one. Requiring all of them to be absent has probability bounded below by a positive constant depending only on \(k\). Requiring one endpoint from each of the \(k-1\) unconditional default edges to be absent then guarantees no selected edge, proving a lower bound of order \(t^{k-1}\). Avoiding these \(k-1\) edge events also gives an upper bound of order \(t^{k-1}\). Therefore multiplication by \(t^{-(3k-1)}\) diverges at order \(t^{-2k}\). This family yields a corner limit and does not give a coefficientwise polynomial border.

## Girth

For any of the bipartite graphs above, subdivide every edge into an odd path of length \(2h+1\), assigning each private vertex probability \(1-t\). An inactive original vertex has zero cover weight, while active and private vertices have weight one. Given the covered old endpoints \(T\), a path needs \(h\) additional private vertices when at least one endpoint is covered and \(h+1\) when neither is. The optimal total weighted cover cost is therefore \(h|E|+\tau_{\rm active}\). To see the lower bound, add one endpoint of each still-uncovered old edge to \(T\); this costs at most one per edge. For the upper bound, use all inactive vertices and a minimum cover of the active graph. Taking scale \(t^{-(h|E|+B)}\) preserves the same Boolean support. An odd subdivision remains bipartite and preserves maximum degree. Choosing a polynomial path length gives girth at least \(N^\alpha\) for any fixed \(0<\alpha<1\).
