# When the degree in the approximation parameter is polynomial

Assume at each input length one fixed bipartite graph of polynomial size and a one-parameter specialization. Every coefficient of every affine probability is a real polynomial in \(\varepsilon\) of degree at most \(D\), with \(D\) polynomial in that input length. Require cube preservation for all sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\) and convergence to finite nonnegative values on the Boolean cube after an arbitrary common positive rescaling \(c(\varepsilon)\).

Under these extra hypotheses the language lies in nonuniform NC. This includes advice of arbitrary real precision in the polynomial coefficients and permits a nonanalytic rescaling. The graph constructions for NL/poly in [the corner example](corner-separation.md) have degree one prior to optional stretching, so
\[
 \mathrm{NL/poly}\ \subseteq\ \{\text{these bipartite Boolean-corner supports}\}\ \subseteq\ \mathrm{NC/poly}.
\]
The general P/poly construction uses exponents of polynomial bit length that can have exponentially large values.

For sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\), the sign of every polynomial slope in every absence probability \(q_v=1-p_v\) is fixed, including identically zero slopes. Orient its corresponding literal once. The constant term of the nonnegative literal expansion is the value at a fixed minimizing Boolean corner, hence still a polynomial of degree at most \(D\). Each literal's coefficient is a signed copy of one slope and likewise has degree at most \(D\). Since every active summand is nonnegative near zero, the valuation of \(q_v(x)\) is the minimum of the active constant or literal valuations, lying in \(\{0,\ldots,D,\infty\}\). The value infinity means identically zero.

The graph's no-edge event is the union, over covers, of the event that all vertices in that cover are absent. At a fixed Boolean input this puts its probability between the largest cover product and the number of covers times that product. Since the probability itself is a polynomial, its valuation is exactly the minimum cover weight. If any target value is positive, choose one such input. There the unscaled probability equals \(a\varepsilon^B+o(\varepsilon^B)\) for \(a>0\) and integer \(B\le ND\) on an \(N\)-vertex graph. Thus a single \(c(\varepsilon)\varepsilon^B\) tends to a positive finite constant, regardless of how \(c\) was originally specified. Finiteness on all corners rules out any smaller exponent. Acceptance is equivalent to the minimum cover weight being at most \(B\). An empty support needs only one advice bit.

For the threshold test replace infinite and larger finite weights by \(B+1\). Store the polynomially many local integer valuations and signed literals as advice; the selected minima are computed in parallel. Give vertex \(v\) as many clones as its weight and connect all clones of opposite original endpoints across each edge. A cover of this blown-up bipartite graph must completely include at least one endpoint group of every edge with both weights positive. Conversely a cover of the weighted graph gives a cover of the same cost in the blowup, so the unweighted cover optimum is its weighted optimum. For a fixed circuit layout reserve \(B+1\) possible clones per vertex and leave surplus clones isolated. A zero-weight vertex contributes an empty, hence completely included, group.

By the bipartite matching-cover theorem it suffices to test whether the maximum matching has size at least \(B+1\) and negate the answer. Pad both sides with isolated vertices to a fixed common order \(m\). For a threshold \(k\le m\), add \(m-k\) dummy vertices per side, connecting each to all original vertices on the other side, with no dummy-to-dummy edges. The augmented graph has a perfect matching precisely when the original graph has a matching of size at least \(k\). Thresholds exceeding the common order are immediate.

Put a different indeterminate on every possible edge of the augmented graph, and zero on each missing edge. The determinant of its bipartite adjacency matrix is a nonzero polynomial exactly when a perfect matching exists: the determinant terms corresponding to different perfect matchings are different monomials. Over a prime field larger than twice the matrix order, a uniform random substitution gives a nonzero determinant with probability at least one half in that case, by the polynomial identity bound. No substitution can create a false positive. Take \(n+2\) independent substitutions, where \(n\) is the original input length. A union bound gives one polynomial-size list of field constants that works for every Boolean input of that length. Hardwire it in the advice. The determinant over each of these fields has polynomial-size, polylog-depth Boolean circuits via Berkowitz's division-free algorithm and elementary modular arithmetic. All remaining selection, input activation and parallel disjunction steps have the same bounds. [Berkowitz, *On computing the determinant in small parallel time using a small number of processors*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0020019084900188)
