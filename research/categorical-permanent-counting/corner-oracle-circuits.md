# Counting on Boolean corners with arbitrarily nested permanent oracles

Suppose a monotone circuit uses nonnegative real constants, variables, addition, multiplication and permanent gates of explicit square orders. It can have arbitrary depth, reuse entire computed outputs and place the same child at several oracle positions. If it counts the Hamiltonian-cycle polynomial or the complete undirected graph's perfect-matching polynomial exactly on Boolean inputs, its explicit size on m graph vertices is at least 2^{Ω(m)}. The argument below strengthens the single-projection argument in the companion tight-cut note. Research priority and outside peer review remain open.

## A feature of a parse

It is enough that the circuit's positive formal monomials have supports precisely the desired target witnesses. Exact corner counting implies this by uniqueness of the multilinear interpolant after replacing every positive exponent by one. Conversely, arbitrary positive coefficients on these supports are allowed in this argument. Interpret each monomial as a parse tree of the circuit. At a sum choose a summand, at a product take both slots and at a permanent select one allowed matching. A constant contributes no input label and a variable contributes its label. Prune nodes or positions that cannot occur in a full positive output parse.

Use one readout for each target vertex. A variable for an undirected matching edge carries its incident edge as a tag at both endpoints. At every vertex of every complete output parse the set of distinct tags consists of one incident edge. For directed tours tag only the arc's tail; each vertex supplies its unique outgoing arc in the tour. More generally the argument holds whenever each complete output parse has exactly one distinct tag at each of finitely many readouts, with each variable carrying at most one tag there. One may recover all stated target coordinates from these readouts by choosing the smaller endpoint for each unordered matching edge.

At each live gate g call a readout flexible if at least two distinct tags for it occur among all positive parses of g. Fix a particular occurrence in an output parse and freeze all the other branches. Every parse of g can be inserted there and still yields an output parse. Thus every parse of g has at most one distinct tag per readout. Moreover, for a flexible readout no frozen exterior branch can carry a tag there, since some gate parse would conflict with it. Every parse of g must consequently carry a tag at such a readout. Define the feature of a gate parse to be the vector of one-hot tags on flexible readouts, and zero on its other readouts.

Fix a possible local choice of a parent gate. For each flexible parent readout, if a selected child slot is flexible then it is the only selected slot able to supply any tag there: child parses in the distinct slots are chosen independently, including slots wired to the same physical child. Its feature therefore carries the parent tag. Otherwise, some selected slot always supplies its unique possible tag, since if all selected slots could omit a tag, their independent choices could leave the parent uncovered. All such mandatory tags are equal. The local choice itself supplies one copy of the corresponding indicator. Every child-flexible readout is flexible at its parent, as the slot participates in some viable choice. These facts give an exact additive identity for each parse:

    parent feature = sum of features of invoked slots + new features of the local choice.

Leaves have no flexible readouts and their features are zero. Their fixed tags were already taken into account when classifying the mandatory slots of their parents. For sums and products each alternative has a fixed new-feature vector and a fixed list of child slots.

## Decode one oracle matching on the common tight-cut formulation

For a permanent gate and a fixed flexible parent readout, color each slot with a flexible child by a fresh color unique to its matrix position. Color each slot whose child always supplies the same tag by that tag; all other positions are neutral. Each matching now contains at least one colored slot and all colored slots in it agree in color, by the preceding two cases. In particular a flexible special position excludes all other colored positions in the selected matching. If the color is a genuine target tag then this oracle locally supplies its indicator; if it is a special color the corresponding child supplies the feature instead.

The tight-cut construction provides one polynomial-size bounded extension of the bipartite matching polytope on which *every* such categorical color indicator is affine, simultaneously with all original edge usages. The extension contains the convex hull of deterministic matching encodings and projects exactly onto their joint incidence-and-color hull. It depends only on the physical bipartite graph, so the number of alternative assignments and the number of tagged target readouts never multiply the required inequalities. Neutral positions, constant entries, repeated child wires and disconnected support graphs are all permitted. Positive affine forms may instead be built out of ordinary gates.

At each physical gate introduce its expected occurrence count. At sums route a nonnegative split, at products route the entire count to both slots, and at a permanent take a homogenized copy of the common extension scaled by that count. Its projected slot usages are child demands. Include each local new-feature map in the global feature sum, multiplying its affine constant by the gate's supply. A node's supply equals its total incoming demands plus one at the root. Counting all the occurrences of an individual parse provides a feasible point. Conversely, for a feasible point a normalized local block of positive supply is a mixture of actual matchings or ordinary gate choices with their correct joint mean features. Sample one independently per reached occurrence, working down the finite DAG. The expected visit count at every gate equals its supply by topological induction. The expected output feature equals the projected LP value by the parse identity. The projected polytope is exactly the convex hull of the root features. The local extensions are bounded, so an unused local block of supply zero can be set to zero and contributes no spurious feature or demand.

For an explicit circuit of size s, the sum of the local bound O(k(q+1)^3) over orders k and edge counts q is polynomial in s. A missing root-flexibility coordinate has a fixed value if it is mandatory at the output; in the complete matching and tour families of sufficiently large order all root readouts are flexible. The known exponential extension complexity of their witness polytopes gives the claimed exponential circuit lower bound. The directed tour polytope projects onto the undirected traveling salesman polytope by summing antiparallel arc coordinates. [Rothvoß](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.2369)

The undirected tour counting version also follows. For each directed vertex create three undirected vertices in a private path from an in-port through a degree-two midpoint to an out-port. Every directed arc i→j becomes a possible undirected edge from out-port i to in-port j. The midpoint forces both private path edges into any tour. Each in-port and out-port therefore takes exactly one external edge. These external choices encode a permutation, and connectedness forces it to be a single directed cycle. Each directed cycle gives exactly one undirected edge-set tour. Set private path edges to one, all extraneous edges to zero and retain the directed variables on the external edges. This is a parsimonious monotone corner projection. Subdividing a forced private edge supplies one or two further vertices if necessary.

## How much corner information suffices

For the nonnegative counting convention, correctness on every Boolean input is stronger than necessary. It suffices to obtain the intended mass on each minimal witness H, reject all H with one member removed, and have the sum of the masses at those witnesses equal the value on the all-ones input. Nonnegativity first excludes every monomial on a proper subset of any H; the distinct minimal witnesses then correspond to disjoint groups of monomials, and the all-ones equality leaves no mass for any other support. For families whose monomials have unit coefficients, the required values are 1 on each H, 0 on each single-edge deletion, and the number of witnesses on the full graph.

The same exponential obstruction applies to a positive border that converges to all the correct corner counts at a fixed circuit size. With ordinary nonnegative-coefficient leaves, boundedness of the outputs at the all-ones corner bounds their values at the midpoint. The finite-dimensional compactness and normalized-permanent factorization in the companion oracle-circuits proof then give a limiting circuit of size O(s) with nonnegative-coefficient leaves. The limit counts exactly on the corners and hence has pure support. This argument requires convergence to arbitrary accuracy at fixed size; it specifies no universal constant tolerance for corner counts alone. The single-fractional-edge note gives a quantitative obstruction to constant-error approximation on a different set of test inputs.

Grochow's Question 5.1 permits any positive output on a graph admitting a matching. Such a circuit may have monomials containing incompatible edges in addition to a complete witness. The exact-on-corners result above relies on support purity. The ordinary Boolean decision problem remains distinct. Prior work proves ordinary monotone counting circuit bounds and develops the distinction between counting and formal equality. The present oracle argument uses the bipartite perfect matching gates through their tight cuts.
