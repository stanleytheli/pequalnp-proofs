# A linear-size extended formulation for circuits with permanent oracle gates

Let C be a finite acyclic circuit with additions, multiplications, nonnegative constants, variables and permanent gates of arbitrary explicit square orders. An entry of a permanent gate can be any previously computed value; entries can repeat and a gate can feed arbitrarily many parents. Write s for the circuit's explicit size, including each ordinary wire and each matrix position at every oracle gate. If the output polynomial P is nonzero, then

    xc(Newt(P)) ≤ O(s).

Consequently the formal Hamiltonian-cycle, unsigned Pfaffian and fixed-q cut polynomials from Grochow's Theorems 4.2–4.5 require exponential total size even with general monotone oracle access to the permanent, without a depth or fanout restriction. The general Newton-polytope formulation applies whenever positive coefficients cannot cancel or annihilate each other, including the nonnegative reals and the formal Boolean semiring. For the three requested target families the ordered-semiring conclusion also follows with the weaker order convention discussed below. The statements below on limits use the real numbers.

This gives an affirmative argument for the formal-polynomial question as posed in Grochow’s Open Question 5.6. Research priority has not been established. Ordinary monotone formulas and tropical circuits have prior connections to extensions and linear programs; both ingredients of the proof here have elementary formulations. Formal polynomial equality is important, including for the Boolean semiring. The associated decision-function and Boolean-corner-counting questions ask for different statements. [Grochow, Question 5.6](https://toc.cs.uchicago.edu/articles/v013a018/v013a018.pdf) [Hrubeš and Yehudayoff, Theorem 35](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol200-ccc2021/LIPIcs.CCC.2021.9/LIPIcs.CCC.2021.9.pdf) [Jukna on tropical circuits and LPs](https://web.vu.lt/mif/s.jukna/tropical/commentB1.html) [Hertrich and Loho on monotone networks and extended formulations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.03006)

For the usual no-cancellation case, monomial support propagates literally. An explicit Boolean semiring quotient handles possible nonzero positive zero-divisors in the weaker order convention. Suppose first that 0<1. In the nonnegative cone call a scalar x live when some nonnegative r satisfies 1≤rx. The scalars 1 and 0 map to true and false. A live product makes each factor live by absorbing the other factor into its witness; conversely multiply two witness inequalities. If one summand is live, their sum is live by monotonicity. Conversely x+y≤2 max(x,y) by totality, so doubling a witness for a live sum makes its larger summand live. This defines a homomorphism to the Boolean semiring. All positive natural multiples of 1 survive. Every coefficient in each of the three target supports is such a multiple. Applying the map to the putative circuit therefore preserves the entire target support. If the order instead has 1<0, any nonnegative coefficient c satisfies c=c·1≤c·0=0, so the only nonnegative scalar is zero. All formal coefficients then belong to the subsemiring generated by 0 and 1. Every positive natural multiple of 1 is strictly below zero in this orientation and therefore nonzero. Support on that subsemiring again maps to Boolean. The one-element semiring is excluded, as it has no nonzero target.

The exponential estimates use the matching and tour polytope bounds of Rothvoß and the cut polytope bound of Fiorini, Massar, Pokutta, Tiwary and de Wolf. The epigraph construction below also fits the graph implementation method for convex functions of Grant and Boyd. ## A formulation using the expected number of gate occurrences

Prune identically zero polynomials. A surviving addition can retain a single child. A surviving product has no zero factor. In a surviving permanent gate forbid matrix positions whose children are zero; its remaining graph admits a perfect matching. Discard unused nodes. These simplifications only require existence; no identity-testing oracle enters the proposed circuit. For monotone formal circuits they can also be carried out structurally.

A monomial occurrence of the output has a parse tree: at an addition choose a child, at a product take both occurrences, and at a permanent choose a perfect matching of its matrix positions and take the respective children. Positive constants contribute exponent zero. Unrolling a shared gate independently each time it occurs is exactly distributive expansion of a formal polynomial. Every full parse supplies a positive coefficient, and every monomial in the support has a parse. The tree can be large; it is used only in the proof.

Make a nonnegative occurrence variable λ_g for each physical gate. Start the output with supply one. For each other gate its supply is the sum of all incoming demands from its parents. At an addition split λ_g arbitrarily among the surviving positions, with nonnegative flows summing to λ_g. At a product send exactly λ_g to each operand slot, including twice to the same child for a square. At an order-k permanent create one nonnegative y_ij for each allowed position. Every row and column sums to λ_g. Send y_ij to the child in that position, adding contributions when children coincide. At each variable leaf its λ counts occurrences of that input. The output vector sums these leaf amounts by variable name. Constants are ignored. This uses O(s) variables, inequalities and equations. Its feasible region is bounded: working from the output backwards, each parent can send only bounded total mass to each slot and the DAG is finite. No bound on the numerical magnitudes of these variables is part of the size estimate.

An individual parse supplies a feasible integral point by counting its plays at the original gates. Conversely, given any feasible point, generate a random parse from the output. At each addition occurrence use its normalized split. At each permanent occurrence with λ_g>0 the matrix y/λ_g is doubly stochastic, with forbidden positions zero; decompose it as a mixture of allowed perfect matchings. Sample from this mixture at each such occurrence. A zero-supply gate is reached with probability zero by the induction below and needs no choice. Products send both children. Topological induction from output to leaves proves that the expected number of occurrences of each gate and each slot is its prescribed flow: the conditional expected amount per occurrence is precisely its local flow divided by its supply. The finite DAG has finite maximal unrolling. The resulting exponent is a finite random vector supported on actual monomials. Its expectation is the claimed projected point. This proves equality with the convex hull of the exponents, including when different matching positions refer to the same child and different parents share an entire subcircuit.

## An equivalent support-function proof

For direction u, the support function of a monotone polynomial's Newton polytope evaluates a variable leaf as u_i, a nonzero constant as zero, addition as a maximum, and multiplication as a sum. The permanent gate evaluates a maximum-weight perfect matching with child values as edge weights. Use one epigraph variable for each gate. At a permanent output g use free real row and column potentials r_i,c_j subject to r_i+c_j≥h_child at each allowed position and h_g≥Σr_i+Σc_j. Assignment LP duality makes this bound attainable at the exact child values. At sums require h_g≥each child, and at products h_g≥h_left+h_right. Induction bounds every feasible epigraph assignment below by the true support value at each gate; simultaneous equality is attainable by choosing exact child values and optimal assignment potentials. This is a second O(s)-size representation, now of the support-function epigraph. The explicit bounded formulation above supplies a direct polytope extension.

For a general oracle with nonnegative formal coefficients a similar construction uses its Newton polytope as the local branching distribution: replace each assignment block by a homogeneous copy of an extension for the Newton polytope of the oracle after zero inputs have been pinned. A local monomial exponent then specifies how many times to call each argument. The equality with a distribution over actual monomials still follows by convex decomposition. Pinning zero arguments retains a coordinate face with exponent zero on those arguments. For a bounded nonempty local extension a zero supply gives only zero projected demands. This gives a bound linear in the total ordinary size and sum of local extension sizes including slot accounting. Circuit depth can be arbitrary.

## Positive limits, affine leaves of mixed slope and numerical approximation

Fix the topology at one target length; any sequence of bounded explicit size has a constant-topology subsequence after numbering its gates. At stage j allow arbitrary affine leaves nonnegative on the real cube, nonnegative constants, and positive output rescaling. Fix the pattern of midpoint-zero gates along a subsequence. A nonnegative affine leaf with zero midpoint is identically zero. By induction the same holds for a zero-midpoint gate, since all node operations are positive combinations of products. Prune them as above. Normalize each remaining gate output by its positive midpoint value.

At a leaf the normalized affine shape lies between zero and two and has a convergent coefficientwise subsequence. At a plus gate the normalized result is a convex combination of its normalized children; take a limit of the two scalar weights. At a product it is their product. At a permanent it is a convex combination of products of normalized children, with the weight of each permutation proportional to the product of its children's midpoint values. All these weights lie in [0,1]. The single-gate matching-face and amplitude argument in the main note reconstructs the positive weights exactly using a subset of matrix positions and one positive scalar per retained position, plus a common constant. Coefficientwise convergence of every normalized child entails that of their fixed finite sums and products. Extract these subsequences simultaneously for all nodes. If the scaled output midpoint has finite limit, the full limit is therefore computed by a circuit of the same kind with cube-nonnegative affine leaves and O(s) extra scalars or multiplications (one per wire or matrix position suffices). A zero-limit scale yields the zero polynomial. Boundedness at one strictly interior point suffices: affines compare by the cube point comparison, and gate induction raises this ratio to at most a finite syntactic degree for fixed topology.

At the origin take the lowest nonzero homogeneous part throughout this fixed circuit. A cube-nonnegative affine leaf yields a positive constant or a nonnegative-coefficient homogeneous linear form. In each case every nonzero resulting leading component is positive at strictly positive inputs. A sum keeps the terms of smallest degree, a product multiplies leading components, and a permanent restricts to its minimum-total-degree matchings. Their union is a matching face by the integral decomposition in the main note. Retain exactly these positions and substitute their leading components. This circuit computes the complete leading homogeneous part with ordinary monotonicity. If each affine leaf has at most d nonzero slopes, expanding it into simple nodes costs O(d+1) per leaf. The total extension bound is O(s plus the number of affine coefficients). Taking d≤m² still gives exponential required size for HC_m or the m-vertex unsigned Pfaffian even under a comparison by two finite positive multiplicative factors in a neighborhood of zero, by the support-function argument in the main note. The cut polynomial need not be homogeneous; the formal-polynomial lower bound above uses its full Newton polytope directly.

## Why a final subtraction changes this oracle bound

Over the reals, the classical VNP-completeness of the permanent supplies a polynomial-size signed projection of it to the Hamiltonian-cycle polynomial. Replace each negative scalar in the projected matrix by t times its absolute value, leaving its other entries unchanged. The permanent of this new matrix is a polynomial in t of degree at most the matrix order k. Its value at t=−1 is the desired polynomial. Interpolation at k+1 nonnegative real t values expresses it as a fixed signed real linear combination of ordinary nonnegative permanent evaluations. Combine positive coefficients into one nonnegative sum and the absolute values of the negative coefficients into a second. One final subtraction suffices, even with just one parallel layer of polynomial-size permanent calls. [Valiant, *Completeness classes in algebra*](https://doi.org/10.1145/800135.804419)

Any exact equality HC=A−B from such polynomial-size nonnegative formal oracle circuits entails unbounded cancellation in the positive cube near zero. Indeed A=HC+B coefficientwise and therefore A≥HC there. If A/HC stayed bounded on any positive neighborhood of zero, the preceding approximation lower bound would force exponential size. More specifically, some nonnegative integer input-weight direction has strict inequality between the minimum weight of a monomial of A and that of HC. Otherwise Newt(A)+R^n_+ equals the corresponding dominant for HC: equality of minima in all nonnegative directions determines these upward-closed polyhedra. Then every exponent of A is a convex combination of HC exponents plus a nonnegative vector. On the positive unit cube its monomial is at most the maximum of those HC monomials, bounding A/HC by the finite sum of coefficients of A. The strict separating direction can be perturbed to have positive rational entries and scaled to positive integers. Along x_i=t^{u_i} the ratio grows at least as a positive constant times t^{-1} as t tends to zero. This quantifies the cancellation needed by this restricted oracle construction and has no implication by itself for Boolean P versus NP.
