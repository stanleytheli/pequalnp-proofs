# A coefficient-preserving circuit transformation

The pure-label argument also gives a constructive statement about formal polynomials: on the categorical supports in the corner-oracle note, replacing every positive exponent by one in the output of a monotone permanent-oracle circuit admits a monotone permanent-oracle circuit with polynomial overhead. Coefficients of all parses with the same support add together, as they must for exact corner counts. This section explains the construction; it does not rely on an extension-complexity lower bound.

## Generate the common lift with a weighted matching circuit

Use the elementary tight-cut factorization from the corner-projection note. Its leaves are simple braces with polynomial-size assignment descriptions or order-at-most-three factors with polynomially many individual perfect matchings. The factors form a tree joined through marker vertices. To see this directly, each split introduces two fresh marker vertices linked by the index of their common crossing edge; an existing marker vertex persists in the child that retains it. Its incident edges and their individual identifiers are preserved there, including edges that become boundary edges. Thus after further splits each previously established marker persists in exactly one descendant leaf on its side. Connecting those two leaves for each split produces a tree (induct on the split tree). A permissible global matching is a tuple of leaf matchings with the same edge identifier at each pair of linked markers. Conversely every such tuple reconstructs exactly one original matching by recursive gluing. If an original edge occurs on several shores, assign its original coefficient or input weight to any one leaf copy. All copies then have the same incidence in a compatible tuple, by the same induction.

Assign a formal Laurent monomial to every edge of a large terminal factor and to every specific matching of each small terminal factor. Ordinary input coefficients or previously computed child polynomials can multiply these weights. One can sum the resulting product over *all compatible tuples* using a polynomial-size monotone circuit with permanent gates and invertible target variables as leaves. Root the factor tree and compute messages upwards. For a factor with a parent link, output, separately for each possible parent edge identifier, the sum of all partial products compatible with choosing that edge at this factor's parent marker. A child message depends only on which edge is chosen at its own marker. Multiply this message into the weight of each edge incident to the corresponding child marker at the current factor. An edge touching two child markers receives two factors. For a large terminal factor compute the permanent of this weighted graph after fixing its parent edge (that edge's weight times the complementary minor). Parallel edges contribute a sum of weights per matrix entry. For a small factor enumerate its polynomially many local matchings consistent with the requested edge, multiplying their weights by all applicable child messages. At the root sum without a fixed parent edge. Every consistent tuple contributes exactly once. Disconnected components can be multiplied. The total number of factors, messages, explicit slots and arithmetic operations is polynomial in the original oracle size. This computation uses one distinct marker at a factor per incident tree link.

Every categorical indicator on the universal lift has an **integral** affine expression in its physical coordinates. At a large brace it is a sum of certain edges or one minus a sum. At a small factor it is a zero-one assignment to its matching indicators. The reference-pair recursion adds such expressions and subtracts the indicator of certain crossing-edge choices. Those boundary indicators are just sums of ordinary incident edges at one surviving marker. Constants can be assigned to a single factor; all bounds are polynomial in the description since each readout appears once in each split branch, and each factor has at most the original number of edges. On a small terminal factor any edge coordinate can be rewritten as the sum of its matching indicators that use it. Thus local Laurent weights can encode target-variable exponents for these affine expressions, including negative integer exponents. Their product at every full matching has the desired nonnegative zero-one feature exponents. The entirely separate slot weights are the polynomials of the corresponding actual children; they retain all counting coefficients.

Now process the original circuit in topological order. For every gate produce a polynomial summing over all its parses, with its original numerical coefficient and the squarefree feature monomial on its flexible readouts. When inserting a child polynomial into a parent slot, use these already corrected values. A parent sum or product adds its fixed local feature monomial separately per alternative. At a permanent use the message circuit just constructed for its local categorical new-feature vector. Its product with the selected child features gives precisely the feature for the full parent parse, by the corner-oracle argument. One may use one output variable per actual target edge instead of two endpoint variables, by recording an undirected edge at its smaller endpoint only. If a readout has a fixed tag on every root parse, append its required variable once at the root as applicable. The intermediate result is a subtraction-free circuit with Laurent monomial leaves; its root polynomial is the intended ordinary multilinearization.

## Eliminate inverses of individual variables

Here is a general lemma over the nonnegative reals. In a circuit with addition, multiplication and permanent gates, allow Laurent monomials at its input leaves. If the final formal polynomial has no negative exponents, the same polynomial has an ordinary monotone permanent-oracle circuit of polynomially larger size in the explicit circuit, the number of coordinates, and the bit lengths of the leaf exponents. Inverses of arbitrary intermediate polynomials are outside this statement.

Prune zero nodes and unusable positions. For every remaining gate polynomial P_g and each coordinate i, let μ_g(i) be the minimum exponent of that coordinate among its monomials. These minima can occur on different monomials. Compute the normalized polynomial x^{−μ_g}P_g at each gate. At a leaf normalize a Laurent monomial to its scalar. At a sum the coordinate minimum is the smaller child minimum; multiply each normalized summand by its nonnegative monomial shift. At a product the two vectors of minima add. At a permanent, for each coordinate independently minimize the sum of the child minima over its allowed perfect matchings. Positive coefficients give the exact resulting coordinate minimum. With integer costs c_e=μ_child(i), take integral dual row/column potentials u_p,v_q attaining this minimum and satisfying u_p+v_q≤c_pq on each edge. Weight each normalized slot by the nonnegative exponent c_pq−u_p−v_q in this coordinate. On any matching the potentials sum to the same optimum μ_g(i), giving exactly the normalized gate polynomial, concurrently in every coordinate. At the root all μ_g(i)≥0; restore the ordinary monomial shift.

For an elementary justification of the integral potentials, choose an optimum assignment σ and impose equality on its matched cells. Write v_{σ(q)}=c_{q,σ(q)}−u_q. The remaining inequalities are u_p−u_q≤c_{p,σ(q)}−c_{q,σ(q)}. Direct an arc q→p with the right side as integer length for each allowed cell. Every directed cycle has nonnegative length, by optimality of σ against reassigning its matched columns on that cycle. Attach a new source with zero-length arcs to every vertex. Its shortest-path lengths give the required integers u; all have magnitude polynomial in the order times the maximum cost magnitude. Output exponents can grow exponentially along the circuit, but their binary lengths stay polynomial because each gate sums at most its arity many preceding minima. Create a usual power by repeated squaring, with cost linear in that bit length. This proves the polynomial bound.

The transformation applies to numerical counting on Boolean inputs when multilinear interpolation implies categorical purity. It also treats any positive weighted distribution of parses on such supports without assuming prescribed numerical coefficients. Its algorithm for choosing reference completions, flexible readouts and weights need only exist for the nonuniform circuit-size claim. These new details are a written proof, subject to independent review.
