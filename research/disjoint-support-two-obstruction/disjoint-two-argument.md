# Excluding disjoint support two in the mixed-row permanent problem

Let a seven-dimensional complex linear subspace of 4 by 5 matrices preserve the rectangular permanent after translation by the all-ones matrix and project injectively onto each pair of rows. Suppose the row ranks are 4,4,4,3 and two rank-four normals have full support. Then **if the third rank-four normal is sparse, it must be supported on a single coordinate.**

The [mixed-row argument](https://github.com/stanleytheli/pequalnp-proofs/tree/b84251b1c4c1701c56b3d1601cce6f0725bcf67e/research/mixed-row-obstruction) excludes sparse supports of size three or four and defines the two nonempty active classes \(A,B\) of the low row. The low physical weights satisfy \(\sum_A x_j=\sum_Bx_j\ne0\). The [containing-support result](https://github.com/stanleytheli/pequalnp-proofs/tree/f816f9528498f076f3a8e6005a118305b8047b48/research/mixed-support-two-obstruction) proves that a sparse support \(S\) of size two must be disjoint from \(A\cup B\). We exclude this final support-two case.

Let \(J=S^c\). The two active classes are subsets of its three indices. Their union has two or three indices, with \(A\) a singleton after exchanging the class names. Each full row has phase one on the active classes. The phase, pair-orientation and projected-rank enumeration of the mixed-row argument gives 1,974 retained orientations with disjoint support two. In 1,484 of them the full/full pair is three-ratio. The existing three-conic finite computation excludes 1,108 by the empty open locus and 376 by the Gram rank obstruction.

The remaining 490 have a two-ratio full/full pair, so every phase is a sign on its support. Normalize the signed sparse total on \(S\) to one. The two sparse/full pairs have either one or two common-ratio pairs; this is checked by the finite enumeration. As in the preceding results, \(F\) denotes the nine physical annihilating covectors and \(K=(3I_4-\mathbf1\mathbf1^t)\otimes(4I_5-\mathbf1\mathbf1^t)\); every candidate has \(\operatorname{rank}(FKF^t)\le6\).

## Two common ratios

There are 394 orientations. Introduce three homogeneous variables: the sparse total and two independent missing weights. The third missing weight is determined by the low balance. Both full normals have constant phase ratio on \(S\), so their signed totals and the two missing-coordinate quadrics depend only on these three variables. Applying the finite dual-space computation to the two quadrics excludes 126 open loci at once. In 262 more, finite aggregate weight operators remain.

To restore the individual weights on \(S\), take their signed masses proportional to \(s+v,s-v\), with twice the aggregate scale elsewhere. The two full and sparse phases are proportional on \(S\); the low covectors and both common-ratio tangent covectors have no active-column derivative. Swapping the two *signed* sparse masses swaps the physical covector columns, leaving the Gram invariant. Its coefficient in \(v^2\) is supported on one column-difference direction in the top three row slots. Their restricted row metric \(3I_3-\mathbf1\mathbf1^t\) has rank two, giving rank at most \(2b\) for the quadratic coefficient in operator dimension \(b\). Full-degree and coprime reduced determinants of projected pencils exclude all complex splits in these 262 cases. This uses the [degree-preserving specialization criterion](https://github.com/stanleytheli/pequalnp-proofs/tree/4ba51579c0716b65d49ec02fa68e51c3e3d92e72/research/support-two-row-obstruction).

The other six orientations all have phase rows \((+,+,+,+,+)\), \((+,+,+,-,-)\), and \((0,0,0,+,+)\). Both missing-coordinate pairs partition \(J\) into \(P,Q\), so \(TP=1\) with \(T=x_0+x_1+x_2\) for both rows. For four orientations \(x_0=x_1=p\); the choices give \(p=1/T\) or \(p=T-1/T\), and \(x_2=T-2p\). For two orientations \(x_0=x_1+x_2=p\); then \(2p^2=1\) and \(x_1\) is a free parameter.

Write \(F_0,F_1,F_2\) for the high normal covectors and \(H\) for the full/full sign-pair covector with components \(-s_0v_0\) in row one and \(s_1v_1\) in row zero. Take on the left of the Gram the two low covectors, both common-pair covectors, the total sum, and
\[
 F_0-F_1+F_2,\qquad H-s_0F_1+s_1F_0 .
\]
Use all nine original covectors on the right. The second displayed combination has zero slope in the active split. The slope of the first is the top-row vector \((1,1,1)\) on the active column difference, which is in the radical of the restricted row metric. All other varying right covectors also have their slopes only in those three row slots. The balanced weights at zero split have equal active columns for each covector, so their cross pairing with a column difference vanishes. The rectangular Gram is therefore independent of the active split. Every seven-column minor must vanish.

For the four rational families, clear the nonzero denominator \(T\); the resulting weights are integral polynomials of degree at most two. The seven-minor has degree at most 56, computed by exact interpolation. For the two quadratic families, represent \(2p\) by the two-dimensional companion of \(X^2-2\); the full row totals and active weights are constant and the remaining covectors are affine in \(x_1\). The 14-row block determinant has degree at most 28. After removal only of required nonzero factors the integer gcd of the minors is one in every case.

## One common ratio

There are 96 orientations. Let \(k\) be the common-ratio full row and let \(c=\pm1\) be its phase ratio to the sparse row on \(S\). For missing coordinates use \(y_j=cE_{kj}x_j\), so that the normalized active full total is one. Let \(t=\sum_J y_j\). The conic is \(t\sum_P y_j=1+\sum_Ry_j\), where \(R=J\setminus(P\cup Q)\). The low balance gives a second fixed linear form in the three \(y_j\), independent of their sum since its coefficients on the two active classes are opposite. Choose a free missing coordinate \(v\); the sum and balance give \(y=C(t)+Lv\), with rational constant \(L\) and affine \(C\). The common conic becomes \(\alpha(t)v=\beta(t)\).

For \(\alpha\ne0\), substitute \(v=\beta/\alpha\) away from the zeros of \(\alpha\). At any zero of \(\alpha\), a candidate must also satisfy \(\beta=0\); all 92 generic cases have constant gcd. In the other four orientations \(\alpha\) vanishes identically and the square-free condition is \(t^2-2=0\). There the missing coordinate \(v\) remains free and the verifier works over the monic quadratic algebra for \(t\).

For the other sparse/full pair let \(h\) be the relative sign on its chosen first group and let \(T'\) be its actual signed missing total. Its two active classes are singleton coordinates. The two-ratio conic gives \(z^2+hT'z=2\), and the signed sparse masses on the two groups are \((2+z)/2\) and \(-z/2\). Multiplying by their respective sparse phases recovers the actual weights. After clearing denominators, the weight operators lie in a monic quadratic tower over the univariate parameter, of degree at most four. The verifier uses the [packed norm and fibre argument](https://github.com/stanleytheli/pequalnp-proofs/tree/f816f9528498f076f3a8e6005a118305b8047b48/research/mixed-support-two-obstruction), with the actual physical tangent covector and ratio sign of each pair. Every common denominator divided out in the generic parametrization is nonzero there. The four exceptional \(t\)-fibres are checked separately. Successive projected norms bound the possible free parameters. Their finite roots are evaluated by companion matrices, nonzero-form restriction and a split-prime Gram certificate. All 96 orientations are excluded. This completes the support-two exclusion.
