# Arbitrary terminal ANDs above one earlier AND

Let G be a finite simple bipartite graph without a four-cycle, with m ≥ 1 edges and minimum vertex-cover size τ. An OR circuit on the raw variables supplies the two operands A,B of a single earlier AND h. Further OR gates on raw variables and h supply both operands of arbitrary terminal ANDs; their disjunction is the output. A,B and each raw OR wire are identified with their sets of variable labels. All OR gates can be shared. If this circuit computes the full edge predicate, its size is at least m + τ − 1. The usual star cover attains this bound. The number of terminal gates is c, the number of inner OR gates is q, and the original size is at least q + 2c.

A safe product of two nonempty raw sums is a star: all cross pairs must be graph edges, so one operand is a singleton. Merge repeated centers by ORing their queries. In a raw-star representation with k distinct centers, the OR gates needed by the raw queries number at least the sum of their neighborhood-support sizes minus k, because a raw gate involving at least two labels can belong to only one center cone. If an edge occurs at two distinct centers, count it twice in this sum.

A useful repair rule starts by setting h to false. Suppose b original terminal terms vanish and c₀ ≤ c−b distinct raw centers remain after merging them. The new raw OR cost q₀ is at most q − δ + c−b−c₀, where δ ≥ 0 and δ ≥ 1 when b=0. In the latter case every used path from h to a terminal has an OR reader before the terminal, so at least one OR disappears. If all missing edges lie in a star at a, and their far endpoints lie in one existing raw wire W, the old size already meets the bound. Project W to N(a) for the count. A wide gate surviving in this projected computation cannot occur in a raw-star cone of a different center (two common neighbors would result). If a is missing, count W's projected cone separately and it costs at least its width minus one. If a is present, include one virtual OR combining its existing raw query and the projection of W; any overlapping gates in these cones get counted only once. Either way q₀ ≥ m−c₀−1 and τ ≤ c₀+1. Therefore q+2c ≥ m+c−1+b+δ ≥ m+τ−1. This projection is a lower-bound argument and never requires a second physical copy of a raw gate.

Put I=A∩B. If a terminal takes h on both of its OR operands, h by itself implies the graph predicate, since it forces that terminal true. Then I is empty and A×B consists of genuine edges, so one raw operand is a singleton. Apply the preceding repair rule: if A={a}, every lost edge contains a and its other endpoint is in B. Empty operands and absorbed or unused gates are immediate. The same repair rule always handles any disjoint singleton operand. An overlapping singleton makes h equal a raw input.

From now on h reaches only one side of each affected terminal. Let K be the union of their opposite raw operand supports. The output is the disjunction of the residual obtained by setting h false and the term (OR A) AND (OR B) AND (OR K), by distributivity; K is used for reasoning and need not be built. Any assignment picking a member of each of A,B,K must therefore contain a genuine edge.

If I has at least two elements, every label of K is a common neighbor of all of I. Thus |K| ≤ 1, and every terminal using h is already raw-centered. The other terminals are raw stars. The earlier raw-centered bound applies. [Proof for one internal AND with shared OR gates](one-internal-and.md)

If I={a}, replace h by x_a, yielding only raw stars and excluding no edge incident to a that the triple product could have supplied. Every z in K neighbors a. An additional pair must have z in A or B. If z∈A∩K, its opposite B is wholly contained in N(z). There can be at most one such z since |B|≥2. Symmetrically at most one vertex of B∩K is productive. They cannot both exist because they and a would form a triangle. Thus there is at most one missing star, centered at z with a single existing opposite operand containing a. If z already centers a residual star, append its group with one OR, paid by removal of h. If z is new, append one star at cost two gates. The residual already represents az, and must represent it at center a since z is new. The new star also represents az, giving one additional unit in the raw-star lower bound and paying for the net one extra gate.

It remains to consider disjoint raw operands of width at least two. The two color classes are independent, so among A,B,K at least one is wholly in each color. Ignore empty K. If K is one of the two chosen pure sets, let U be the other pure set, which is an existing raw operand. Its opposite raw operand is V. Because U∩V is empty, the true two-element inputs of the triple are precisely U×(K∩V). The second factor has at most one element by four-cycle freeness. The one-star repair rule applies.

Otherwise A and B themselves are opposite-color sets, say A consists of points and B of lines. The true pairs are (A∩K)×B and A×(B∩K). Each intersection has at most one element. With at most one nonempty intersection, use the one-star repair. Suppose both intersections are present, with A∩K={p} and B∩K={z}. Then B⊆N(p) and A⊆N(z). Restore these two raw groups at p,z. Write n for how many of p,z are new centers. The raw-star network uses at most q₀+2−n OR gates in its queries, and c₀+n centers. Edge pz appears twice. If b≥1 or n≤1, the original q+2c is large enough: more explicitly, k duplicate edges give

    q+2c ≥ m+c+b+δ+k−2,
    τ ≤ c₀+n ≤ c−b+n.

With k≥1 and δ≥1 when b=0 this proves q+2c ≥ m+τ−1 in the indicated cases.

There is one possible extra unit to pay if b=0 and n=2. Choose a terminal whose opposite raw partner V contains p. If V={p}, choose p as a residual center and use the preceding case. Otherwise V has at least two members. Since its residual product is nonempty, its other raw operand is a singleton {b′}, and V consists entirely of points adjacent to b′. Choose a second point t∈V distinct from p and a line b∈B distinct from z. Since z is adjacent to all of A, some u∈A is not adjacent to b, or else a four-cycle would result. The triple input {u,b,t} must contain an edge, and both u and t are points, so bt is an edge. The points p,t are both neighbors of b and b′. Four-cycle freeness forces b=b′. Thus the residual star at b already represents pb, and the new star at p represents pb again. This is a second duplicate distinct from pz. Taking k≥2 in the displayed inequality settles the final case.
