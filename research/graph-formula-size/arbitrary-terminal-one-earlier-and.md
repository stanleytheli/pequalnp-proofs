# Arbitrary terminal ANDs above one earlier AND

Let G be a finite simple graph without triangles or four-cycles, with m ≥ 1 edges and minimum vertex-cover size τ. Allow at most one earlier AND h. An OR circuit on the raw variables supplies its two operands A,B when it is present. Further OR gates on raw variables and h supply both operands of arbitrary terminal ANDs; their disjunction is the output. A,B and each raw OR wire are identified with their sets of variable labels. All OR gates can be shared. If this circuit computes the full edge predicate, its size is at least m + τ − 1. The usual star cover attains this bound. When h is present and used, the number of terminal gates is c, the number of inner OR gates is q, and the original size is at least q + 2c. This counts the earlier AND, the c terminal ANDs and the c−1 ORs needed to combine them. Unused gates may be omitted, as may OR gates whose two inputs are the same physical wire. Free constants can be propagated before this count.

In a product of two nonempty raw sums that implies the edge predicate, the operands are disjoint and every cross pair is an edge. Four-cycle freeness makes one operand a singleton, so the product is a star. Merge repeated centers by ORing their queries. In a raw-star representation with k distinct centers, the OR gates needed by the raw queries number at least the sum of their neighborhood-support sizes minus k, because a raw gate involving at least two labels can belong to only one center cone. If an edge occurs at two distinct centers, count it twice in this sum. In particular this proves the stated circuit bound when the earlier AND is absent, by adding k terminal ANDs and k−1 output ORs.

A useful repair rule starts by setting h to false. Suppose b original terminal terms vanish and c₀ ≤ c−b distinct raw centers remain after merging them. The new raw OR cost q₀ is at most q − δ + c−b−c₀, where δ ≥ 0 and δ ≥ 1 when b=0. In the latter case every used path from h to a terminal has an OR reader before the terminal, so at least one OR disappears. If all missing edges lie in a star at a, and their far endpoints lie in one existing raw wire W, the old size already meets the bound. Project W to N(a) for the count. A wide gate surviving in this projected computation cannot occur in a raw-star cone of a different center (two common neighbors would result). If a is missing, count W's projected cone separately and it costs at least its width minus one. If a is present, include one virtual OR combining its existing raw query and the projection of W; any overlapping gates in these cones get counted only once. Either way q₀ ≥ m−c₀−1 and τ ≤ c₀+1. Therefore q+2c ≥ m+c−1+b+δ ≥ m+τ−1. This projection is a lower-bound argument and never requires a second physical copy of a raw gate.

Put I=A∩B. If a terminal takes h on both of its OR operands, h by itself implies the graph predicate, since it forces that terminal true. Then I is empty and A×B consists of genuine edges, so one raw operand is a singleton. Apply the preceding repair rule: if A={a}, every lost edge contains a and its other endpoint is in B. Empty operands and absorbed or unused gates are immediate. The same repair rule always handles any disjoint singleton operand. An overlapping singleton makes h equal a raw input.

From now on h reaches only one side of each affected terminal. Let K be the union of their opposite raw operand supports. The output is the disjunction of the residual obtained by setting h false and the term (OR A) AND (OR B) AND (OR K), by distributivity; K is used for reasoning and need not be built. Any assignment picking a member of each of A,B,K must therefore contain a genuine edge.

If I has at least two elements, every label of K is a common neighbor of all of I. Thus |K| ≤ 1, and every terminal using h is already raw-centered. The other terminals are raw stars. The earlier raw-centered bound applies. [Proof for one internal AND with shared OR gates](one-internal-and.md)

If I={a}, replace h by x_a, yielding only raw stars and excluding no edge incident to a that the triple product could have supplied. Every z in K neighbors a. An additional pair must have z in A or B. If z∈A∩K, its opposite B is wholly contained in N(z). There can be at most one such z since |B|≥2. Symmetrically at most one vertex of B∩K is productive. They cannot both exist because they and a would form a triangle. Thus there is at most one missing star, centered at z with a single existing opposite operand containing a. If z already centers a residual star, append its group with one OR, paid by removal of h. If z is new, append one star at cost two gates. The residual already represents az, and must represent it at center a since z is new. The new star also represents az, giving one additional unit in the raw-star lower bound and paying for the net one extra gate.

Suppose A and B are disjoint and both have size at least two. A true pair for the triple must contain one label from A and one from B, with one of them in K. Thus its true pairs are precisely (A∩K)×B and A×(B∩K). Each intersection has at most one element, since two distinct members of the intersection would share two neighbors in the opposite operand. If either intersection is empty, use the one-star repair rule. Otherwise write A∩K={p} and B∩K={z}. Then B⊆N(p), A⊆N(z) and pz is an edge.

Restore the two raw groups at p and z using the existing raw wires B and A. Write n for how many of p,z are new centers. The raw-star network uses at most q₀+2−n OR gates in its queries, and c₀+n centers. Edge pz appears at least twice across the restored groups. The raw-star count and the bound on q₀ give

    q+2c ≥ m+c+b+δ−1,
    τ ≤ c₀+n ≤ c−b+n.

If b≥1, then 2b+δ≥n. The same inequality holds if b=0 and n≤1 because δ≥1. In both cases the desired q+2c ≥ m+τ−1 follows.

Suppose b=0 and both p,z are new. Choose a terminal for which p is in the operand opposite h, and another for which z is in the operand opposite h. If either opposite operand is a singleton, choose p or z as an existing center in the residual and apply the preceding count with n≤1. Thus both chosen opposite operands have width at least two. The two residual products are nonempty because b=0. Each is a raw star, so its other operand must be a singleton adjacent to every member of its opposite operand.

At least two different OR gates have h as a direct input. To prove this, suppose there is just one, with output h OR W. With b=0 no terminal has h as a direct input. Removing unused gates and duplicate-input buffers ensures the other input W is nonempty and independent of h. Indeed, a path from h to W would pass through this same unique reader and create a directed cycle. Every OR signal carrying h therefore also carries every raw label of W after h is set to false. In particular W is contained in both singleton receiving operands of the chosen terminals. Its label must neighbor both p and z, which is impossible since pz is an edge and G is triangle-free. This proves the two-reader claim. Both OR gates disappear on setting h to false, giving δ≥2=n. The same gate count closes the proof.
