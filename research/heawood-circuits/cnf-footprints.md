# How many edges can one safe conjunction detect?

Consider a conjunction of k disjunctions of raw Heawood vertex variables. Call it safe if every accepted vertex set contains a graph edge. Its positive footprint consists of the 2-vertex inputs it accepts. With two, three, four and five clauses the maximum footprint sizes are respectively 3, 5, 6 and 7. A safe conjunction which accepts 20 of the graph's 21 edges already requires at least 37 clauses. There is a safe 37-clause conjunction accepting all 21.

## Two exact formulations

Fix any graph G and a set D of edges required to be accepted. A clause must meet each edge of D, so its variables form a vertex cover of D. The whole conjunction is safe exactly when every maximal independent set of G misses some clause. Thus the minimum clause count is a set-cover optimum. The universe consists of the maximal independent sets I, and a variable set W covering D covers exactly those rows I disjoint from W.

Equivalently color the maximal independent sets. Two rows I,J conflict if their union contains an edge of D. A clause that both rows miss cannot meet that edge, so the least number of clauses is at least the chromatic number of this conflict graph. Conversely form one clause from all variables absent from every row in its color class. It meets every required edge: if its two endpoints appeared somewhere in the same color, two rows in that color would conflict (or one would contain an edge). It misses every row of that color. This gives exact equality.

The Heawood graph has 37 maximal independent sets. The set-cover calculation in footprints.cpp and the separate coloring calculation in coloring.cpp agree on all 2,097,151 nonempty required edge subsets, grouped into 7,139 orbits under 336 graph symmetries. The columns in footprints.txt are the minimum clause count, the number of required edges, and the number of labeled subsets with those values. For a budget k, take the maximum edge count over all rows with first column at most k. Both algorithms share their board and orbit listing.

The high-end endpoint has a short certificate. The union of any two distinct rows among the 37 contains at least two edges. Hence even after omitting one chosen edge, every pair of rows conflicts, so they require 37 colors. Taking complements of the 37 maximal independent sets gives a full 37-clause formula. CnfFootprint.lean checks the two-formulation equivalence and the 37-clause lower bound for arbitrary set-valued clauses, directly on the 14 vertices and specified 21 incidences.

## Why three clauses can only detect one double star

For a graph with no triangles or four-cycles, every safe conjunction of three sums accepts edges contained in a single star or two stars centered at adjacent vertices. In fact its entire nonempty edge footprint is itself a star or a double star. Encode a vertex by the subset of the three clauses containing it. No vertex has type 123, since the conjunction must reject singleton inputs. A pair is accepted exactly when its two types cover 123.

If all types are singletons or empty there is no accepted pair. If the only doubleton type present is 12, then the accepted pairs form the complete bipartite graph from type 12 to type 3. It is a star if nonempty. Three different doubleton types would form a triangle. In the remaining case relabel the two doubleton types as 12 and 13. Each 12 vertex connects to every 13 vertex and every 3 vertex. Each 13 vertex also connects to every 2 vertex. These are all the accepted pairs. If each doubleton group contains one vertex, the resulting graph is a double star. If type 12 contains two or more vertices, then the union of 13 and 3 contains at most one, by four-cycle freeness. The unique type 13 vertex then centers the whole footprint. The other case follows by symmetry.

For a degree-three graph this gives at most five edges. Equality is attained on the Heawood graph by the double star at an edge uv: let the three sums correspond to the doubleton types at u and v and give their other four neighbors the complementary singleton types. Every triple accepted without using u or v would need all three singleton types, but the construction uses only two.

There is a circuit consequence even when terminal AND gates have arbitrary inputs. Suppose there is one internal nonlinear signal h=(OR A) AND (OR B) that can fire on an independent set, all other internal signals are ORs, and the final layer is an OR of safe AND gates. An input to a final AND is a raw OR R, possibly also ORed with h. The two operands cannot both contain h, since then h alone would imply a safe output. Distributing each final product separates the raw-only part D₂ from terms of the form h AND (OR T). Their union is one product h AND (OR C), C the union of the partner sets T. It is safe. Thus setting h to false loses edges from at most one double star, at most five edges for Heawood. This conclusion is about non-reusable positive inputs of h's context, and does not by itself exclude a 26-gate circuit.
