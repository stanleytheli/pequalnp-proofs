# The generic permanent of a bipartite sextet graph

Let a finite simple cubic bipartite graph G have n vertices on each side. Write Z_G for the sum of its perfect-matching monomials in independent edge variables. Suppose a family of such graphs has girth at least γ log₂ n for some fixed γ>0, and edge expansion at least 1/(log n)^C for some fixed C. There is a constant δ>0 such that, for all sufficiently large n, Z_G has a zero/one/variable projection onto the permanent of an m-by-m matrix for m=⌊n^δ⌋. The projection and all routing data can be found deterministically in time polynomial in the size of G.

In particular, take the connected Biggs–Hoare sextet graph S(p) for each prime p≡9 (mod 16). With N=p(p²−1)/24 vertices its girth exceeds (4/3) log₂ N−2. Its generic perfect-matching polynomial has both exact arithmetic-circuit size and Laurent-approximate circuit size at least 2^{Ω((log N)^(4/3))} in every fixed characteristic other than two. This conclusion concerns S(p) itself, with its full symmetry and without attaching another graph.

## Routing directed paths through a contracted perfect matching

Choose a perfect matching M, which exists by Hall's theorem since G is regular bipartite. Contract its n edges. Direct every other edge from the contracted pair containing its left endpoint to the pair containing its right endpoint. The resulting digraph D has indegree and outdegree two. It has no loops. For large n it has no antiparallel pair, since this would give a four-cycle of G. The underlying graph of D has maximum degree four and girth at least half the girth of G. In fact every simple undirected cycle of D lifts to a simple cycle of G by joining the two incident off-matching edges inside each contracted vertex, using its matching edge when their endpoints differ. Each contracted vertex is visited once, and the resulting cycle is at most twice as long.

For every S⊆V(D) of size at most n/2, both its forward closed neighborhood and its backward closed neighborhood have size at least (1+η)|S|, with η≥1/(2(log n)^C) under the displayed expansion hypothesis on G. Indeed, the two physical vertices in every pair in S form a set of 2|S|≤n vertices in G. Its boundary edges are exactly the arcs of D entering or leaving S. The counts in the two directions coincide because D is Eulerian. Each outside vertex accounts for at most two outgoing boundary arcs and at most two incoming boundary arcs.

Fix a positive δ<min(1/50,γ/50), write m=⌊n^δ⌋, let l=⌈log₂ m⌉, set s=4l and R=l+2s=9l. All estimates below concern sufficiently large n. Greedily choose m centers whose undirected radius-R balls are disjoint. Each choice excludes at most 1+4∑_{j<2R}3^j ≤ 4^{2R+1} other centers, and m·4^{2R+1}<n for this δ. These balls are trees since 2R+1 is smaller than the girth of the underlying graph of D. At each center both the forward-directed and the backward-directed trees out to depth R are full binary trees. Their first edges are disjoint and the underlying ball is a tree, so they meet only at their center.

At each center i designate m−1 distinct vertices of depth l in its forward tree, one port for each demand i→j, j≠i. Designate distinct depth-l ports in the backward tree at each center j for its incoming demands i→j. Root-to-port parts in a common tree are allowed to share initial segments. Beyond distinct ports all relevant trees are disjoint. The protected set P consists of the root-to-port skeletons and the next s levels below every designated port, in both orientations. It satisfies |P|=O(m²2^s). Each port has 2^s disjoint full binary descendant trees of height s, rooted at the last protected level. Each such tree has 2^s depth-R leaves. The backward-oriented statements use the direction toward the port.

Process the m(m−1) demands in any order. Each chosen link runs from its own forward port to its own backward port. Let U be the union of all vertices of already chosen port-to-port links. At any stage |U|=O(m²(R+η^{-1} log n)), as follows by the path-length estimate below. In particular |U|<2^{s−1}. For the current forward port discard each of its 2^s descendant trees that meets U. The trees are disjoint, so more than half survive. All these trees are reachable from the port along its protected subtree. No previous link used those first s protected levels because they belong solely to this port. The surviving trees furnish a set A of at least 2^{2s−1} available leaves. The analogous backward port furnishes at least this many leaves B leading to its port. Both sets avoid P∪U.

Remove F=P∪U from D. Then |F|=O(m²2^s + m²(R+η^{-1} log n)). The bounds on η and s imply 2^{2s−1} > 2|F|/η. Starting from A in the remaining graph, forward closed neighborhoods therefore grow by a factor at least 1+η/2 while they have at most n/2 vertices. Each step loses at most |F| of the neighbors supplied by D. Within O(η^{-1} log n) steps this set contains more than n/2 vertices. Growing backward from B gives the same conclusion. The two grown sets intersect, giving an A-to-B path with O(η^{-1}log n) arcs. Prepend the available path from the forward port and append the available path into the backward port. Erasing directed loops produces a simple port-to-port link of length O(R+η^{-1} log n). The new link avoids all earlier links, and its only protected vertices belong to its own two port subtrees. This verifies the size bound on U inductively and allows all demands to be routed.

## Unique surviving extensions and the permanent

In G retain the matching M and precisely the off-matching edges corresponding to the two root-to-port skeletons at each center and all the directed links. Set all other edge variables to zero. Give the matching edge at center i the label X_ii. In the link for i→j give one arc the label X_ij, and give unit weight to its remaining arcs, all skeleton arcs, and all noncentral edges of M.

A perfect matching surviving this specialization is a collection of vertex-disjoint directed cycles in the retained subgraph of D, with M used on the complementary pairs. Every nonempty cycle passes through a center. It follows the outgoing tree to a port, the private link to the incoming tree of another center, then the unique path to that center. Thus the cycles give vertex-disjoint cycles of indices of lengths at least two, encoding a permutation π with the other indices fixed. Conversely, each π has exactly one extension. At a used center only a single in-path and out-path are required and at different centers the trees are disjoint. The links themselves are mutually vertex-disjoint. A fixed center uses its M edge; it does not lie on another link. The resulting monomial is exactly ∏ᵢ X_i,π(i). This proof works over every commutative semiring. A breadth-first search implements the growth and routing, and any bipartite matching algorithm supplies M.

The already machine-checked zero/one/variable projection theorem preserves the same exact and Laurent-size budget. Substituting m=⌊n^δ⌋ into the pinned permanent lower bound preserves its (log n)^(4/3) exponent. The new directed routing and sextet corollary in this note are mathematical arguments. Their full Lean formalization is separate from the checked subcubic and reservoir constructions.

## Why the hypotheses hold for S(p)

The Biggs–Hoare prime-field subfamily with p≡9 (mod 16) is connected, cubic, bipartite and has p(p²−1)/24 vertices. The group PGL₂(F_p) acts transitively by graph automorphisms. For this subfamily the sextets use ordinary projective points over F_p. Weiss's girth bound on bipartite sextets is log₂ N < 3g/4 + 3/2. The code in `checks/sextet.py` constructs this graph from the three projective images defining neighbors of a sextet. It verifies all degrees, both colors, connectivity, order and the girths 14 and 22 at p=41 and p=73. [Hoare, The sextet construction, Chapter 3, especially the five families on p. 77](https://repository.royalholloway.ac.uk/file/701852fb-a2c8-495b-8548-868bd1338531/1/10097512.pdf). [Weiss, Girths of bipartite sextet graphs](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02579225).

To bound the diameter, let v be one vertex and let A consist of all elements moving v at distance at most one. This set is symmetric and contains the stabilizer. It generates PGL₂(F_p): inductively traverse any vertex path by multiplying on the right by suitable elements of A, then use the stabilizer to reach a given group element instead of just its image of v. Every word of length d in A gives a walk of length at most d in S(p). Helfgott proves a uniform polylogarithmic bound for the diameter of SL₂(F_p) with respect to every generating set. The same holds in PSL₂ by lifting generators and adding the kernel. It also holds in PGL₂: its index-two subgroup PSL₂ has Schreier generators of length at most three in A, obtained with one length-one coset representative. This proves diam S(p)≤(log N)^O(1). [Helfgott, Growth and generation in SL₂(ℤ/pℤ)](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2008/167-2/p06).

In an arbitrary connected vertex-transitive graph on N vertices of diameter d, send a unit between each ordered pair along a shortest path and average this collection over its automorphism group. The total vertex load is at most N²(d+1), so every vertex receives load at most N(d+1). Every path between S and its complement visits a boundary vertex outside S. Since there are |S|(N−|S|) such ordered pairs in this direction, |∂S|≥|S|/(2(d+1)) when |S|≤N/2. This implies the needed edge-expansion bound. A spectral expansion theorem for the sextet graphs is unnecessary.

Finally this gives a polynomial-size family indexed by arbitrary sufficiently large dense dimensions, if desired. For a requested dimension k choose a prime p≡9 (mod 16) of size Θ(k^{1/(3δ)}), with a sufficient fixed leading constant so that ⌊(N/2)^δ⌋≥k. Such a prime can be found by enumeration since the prime number theorem for the fixed progression provides a prime between x and 2x for all large x. The graph order and deterministic construction time are polynomial in k for fixed δ, and setting the extra principal block to the identity restricts the larger permanent to dimension k. The graph order N itself still ranges over sextet graph sizes.

## Related matching-minor results

Contracting a fixed perfect matching and orienting the remaining edges is a standard bridge between bipartite matching minors and directed butterfly minors. Our two oriented trees and disjoint links form a model of the complete symmetric digraph on m indices, hence a matching-minor model of K_{m,m} in the original graph. Giannopoulou, Kreutzer and Wiederrecht prove the planar matching-minor exclusion and bounded perfect-matching-width equivalence and its counting algorithm. Giannopoulou, Thilikos and Wiederrecht treat exclusion of a single-crossing matching minor and establish counting hardness on certain matching-minor-closed classes. Giannopoulou and Wiederrecht describe the local structure under K_{t,t} matching-minor exclusion via a flat-wall theorem. Here the conclusions are quantitative in the graph order, a constructive model of K_{⌊n^δ⌋,⌊n^δ⌋} in each graph satisfying the two hypotheses, and an edge-variable polynomial identity with one surviving extension per dense permutation. The unrestricted exact and Laurent circuit bounds come from the pinned algebraic theorem. [Planar matching minors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00703). [Single-crossing matching minors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09348). [Flat-wall theorem for matching minors](https://pure.kaist.ac.kr/en/publications/a-flat-wall-theorem-for-matching-minors-in-bipartite-graphs/).
