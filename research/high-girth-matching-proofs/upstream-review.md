## Meaning

The formal model is a nonuniform division-free straight-line program over formal multivariate polynomials. Every copy of a field constant or input variable appearing as a gate counts. Binary addition and multiplication may reuse prior gates, including the same prior gate twice. Output is any of the first s gates. A polynomial identity over a finite field is an equality of formal polynomials, which is stronger than equality as functions on the finite grid.

The two quantities in the main bound are the Hamiltonian-cycle polynomial over every field and the permanent outside characteristic two. The theorem supplies one real c>0, then one dimension threshold for each characteristic, then quantifies over every field in that characteristic and every larger dimension. Exact and approximate size each exceed m^(c (log₂ m)^(1/3)). The formal approximation model is a circuit with constants in the Laurent-series field F((ε)); in every polynomial coefficient the negative-order terms must vanish and the order-zero term must equal the target. Positive-order terms are unrestricted. There are no finite-precision input distributions or probabilistic approximations in this definition.

VP and VNP are the nonuniform arithmetic families in Statement.lean. VP bounds variable count, formal total degree, and circuit size by n^a+a. VNP is an exponential Boolean-vector sum of a VP family; its witness length is automatically bounded through the variable count. Solution proves the strict inclusion VP ⊊ VNP and that VNP contains a family outside algebraic VP closure in every field. The repository explicitly describes the proof as AI-produced and without human mathematical review at release.
