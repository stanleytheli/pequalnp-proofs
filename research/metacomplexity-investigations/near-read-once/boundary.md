# Critical rows and the cost of verifying an explicit graph function

Let F be a monotone target and C a monotone competitor. Positive equality at all minterms of F and negative equality at all maximal false inputs are necessary and sufficient for equivalence. Each row is individually necessary in a universal black-box checking set. For a maximal false point I, the monotone function F OR the conjunction of the bits of I differs just there. For a minimal true point M, F AND the disjunction of the bits outside M is the corresponding single-point downward flip. Thus an a priori truth-table check can be much larger than a succinct description. For an edge predicate of a finite simple graph these are exactly its edges and maximal independent sets. This accounts for the 40 critical rows (16 edges and 24 maximal independent sets) used by the eleven-vertex desk search.

Even a disjoint matching illustrates the verification cost. Given a Boolean CNF formula Φ on n variables, create one two-vertex matching edge for each variable, labelled with its positive and negative literal. Write F for the edge predicate and Ψ for the monotone interpretation of Φ on the 2n literal wires. AND into Ψ one clause demanding at least one endpoint of each matching edge. Define the monotone competitor C=F OR Ψ. False positives C=1, F=0 select exactly one endpoint of each edge and satisfy Φ, and conversely. This bijection proves #P-completeness of counting false positives against a matching target for a supplied monotone circuit, under parsimonious reductions. Membership in #P follows by guessing the input bits and evaluating both descriptions once. Every false positive here is a maximal independent set. The statement uses general succinct monotone competitors and makes no size-threshold hypothesis. Simple equivalence is already coNP-hard for related matching-plus-monotone-CNF input descriptions. [Kozachinskiy, 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.03815)
