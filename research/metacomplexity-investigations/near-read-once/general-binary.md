# Recognizing arbitrary binary read-once functions from a circuit of bounded sharing

Let a trimmed, single-output Boolean circuit present an arbitrary function on N≥2 essential inputs. Every gate may use any of the sixteen binary Boolean operations, and fanout is free. Write b for the number of distinct wire producers with more than one outgoing operand slot, counting both shared primary inputs and shared gate outputs. Repeated operands count as two slots. There is an algorithm deciding whether the minimum circuit size over this full basis is N−1 in time

    2^(2^O(b)) · poly(N+g),

where g is the size of the supplied circuit. It can also recover an optimum formula. In particular b≤g−N+1. The sparse-ETH reduction in `recognition.md` rules out 2^o(b) poly(N+g) time even for monotone presentations. The singly exponential six-signature algorithm in that note exploits monotonicity; the general theorem has a larger upper bound.

## A split into two independent subfunctions

Restrict some input variables to constants. Let the resulting function h depend on all of the remaining set U. Write a candidate nontrivial split U=A⊔B as a Boolean matrix, indexing rows by assignments on A and columns by assignments on B. There is a binary root h=G(h₁(A),h₂(B)) if and only if the matrix has at most two different rows and at most two different columns. One direction follows by grouping assignments by the output of each child. For the converse, use the two row and column classes as child labels. The entries are constant on each product of classes, so their four values define G.

Essentiality and nonempty A,B force exactly two row classes and exactly two column classes. Choose a B-assignment y₀ where the two row classes disagree. Then h(·,y₀) is a binary label for those classes. A corresponding A-assignment x₀ makes h(x₀,·) a binary label for the column classes. Both labels depend on all variables on their respective sides since h does. The children are thus actual restrictions of h, and the labels may each be complemented by absorbing the sign in G.

Search for any such split. If found, recurse on its two child restrictions. A read-once function always has a root split. Every restriction of a read-once function is still read-once after removing constant inputs and absorbing unary effects into adjacent gate labels. Thus choosing **any** successful split retains a valid decomposition on a YES instance. Conversely a completed recursive decomposition supplies the claimed read-once formula. At a one-variable leaf a signed bit can be absorbed into its parent binary gate. The root has at least two variables. There are at most 2N−1 recursive calls and all use the original circuit with additional fixed inputs.

## Finding the split in bounded-parameter time

An explicit tree of bags supports all local gate constraints. Use one bag for every wire. A gate bag holds its output and its two operands, and each primary bag holds its input. Add all b shared wires to every bag. For each nonoutput wire choose one of its readers and attach its bag to that reader's bag. Acyclicity and trimming show that these parent links form a tree ending at the output. Every nonshared wire occurs in only its own bag and the single adjacent reader bag. Each shared wire occurs everywhere. The largest bag has at most b+3 entries. Binary-branch the bag tree using duplicate copies of a bag at joins if necessary. This yields a width-(b+2) decomposition with polynomially many bags.

For fixed A,B, at least three distinct rows have a constant-length witness. Choose row assignments a₁,a₂,a₃, and a distinguishing column bᵢⱼ separately for each of the three pairs. The six evaluations h(aᵢ,bᵢⱼ), h(aⱼ,bᵢⱼ) must disagree within each pair. For each primary input the equalities among its six values depend only on its side of the split. A fixed input must have its prescribed bit in all six. At each gate, six independent copies of its truth table constrain the six-tuples on its output and its operands. At the root check the three disagreements. A column violation is described by interchanging the roles of A and B. This uses only unary tests and constraints on a gate's three-wire bag.

For clarity, one can implement the split search by a finite-state dynamic program rather than rely on a black-box logical decision procedure. Label every input in every bag by its potential side A or B (pinned inputs carry a separate fixed label). Consistency of those side labels between adjacent bags is checked on their overlap. For **fixed** labels a bottom-up table tracks the subset of valuations of the six-tuples of bits on the current bag that extend to a valid witness below it. There are at most 2^(6(b+3)) individual valuations. At a join, a valuation extends exactly when its restriction to each child overlap matches an accepting child valuation and its local gate constraints hold. Keep two such tables for row and column violations. The root tests the required three disagreements. A fixed split is valid precisely when both witness tables at the root are empty after enforcing the output tests.

To **find** side labels, store at each bag every attainable pair of witness tables, the boundary's side labels, and two bits recording which sides occur below. Combine child states only with consistent side labels; for each resulting choice, calculate both complete witness tables just described. The number of states is at most 2^(2^O(b)). Trying pairs of child states preserves this bound up to constants in the exponent. Forgetting wires and adding labels also stays within the bound. Require both sides to occur and both violation tables to be empty at the root. Backpointers recover a suitable partition. Standard witness-table versions with fewer copies of the circuit recover x₀,y₀ and representatives of both child classes within the same time bound. Evaluating the four representative pairs gives G. The same decomposition works after any restriction, since pins are merely input labels.

Alternatively the entire split test is expressible in monadic second-order logic on the bounded-width labeled circuit. The side and the finitely many complete evaluation sets are vertex-set variables, and gate semantics and membership in either side are local formulas. Bounded-treewidth model checking followed by self-reduction also gives fixed-parameter tractability. [Arnborg, Lagergren and Seese, Easy Problems for Tree-Decomposable Graphs](https://doi.org/10.1016/0196-6774(91)90006-K)

`split_check.py` independently checks the root-split characterization and safe recursion against enumeration of every four-variable truth table and all read-once binary formulas on their supports. Its exhaustive small-instance verifier uses explicit truth tables. The double-exponential tree-decomposition algorithm above is a written construction.

The construction also works for a supplied width-w tree decomposition of the undirected wire graph without reference to sharing. Enlarge every bag by both operands of every gate it contains. A wire's new bag set is its old connected subtree together with the connected subtrees of its consumers; each such subtree met its own at a bag containing the corresponding wire-graph edge. All new occurrences therefore stay connected. The enlarged width is at most 3(w+1)−1. The same argument gives 2^(2^O(w)) polynomial time. Matrix-factor decompositions themselves are classical Boolean functional decomposition.
