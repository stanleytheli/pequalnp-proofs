# Membership queries at the read-once circuit threshold

A query gives the value at one Boolean input of a function on N named, essential variables. The threshold is N−1 binary gates. Under a monotonicity promise the deterministic and bounded-error randomized worst-case query complexities are both Θ(3^(N/3)). The lower bound permits just two exact monotone circuit sizes. With arbitrary Boolean targets, the complexities are Θ(2^N), even when every target has a linear-size circuit. Computation between queries is unrestricted.

## One added term, an exact gate gap

Partition the N variables into t≥2 blocks of sizes d_i≥2. Let F be the OR of the t block conjunctions. For each choice I containing all but one variable from every block, let F_I be F OR the conjunction of all variables in I. There are M=∏ d_i such choices. I is a maximal false input for F, so the only difference between F and F_I is at I itself. Both functions use all N inputs. For F_I this follows from the old block minterms, which remain minimal because I meets at least two blocks. The functions admit monotone formulas with at most two occurrences of every variable.

F has exact size N−1 over both the arbitrary binary and monotone binary bases. The exact monotone binary **circuit** size of every F_I is N+t−2. To see the upper bound, write c_i for the omitted bit in block i and y_i for the conjunction of its d_i−1 selected bits. Compute the usual baseline as the OR of the products y_i c_i, with each y_i already available. In the first product replace c_1 by the OR of c_1 and the conjunction of y_2 through y_t. This adds t−1 gates. For the lower bound, I itself is a minterm. Let Z contain one selected variable from each block. Setting all of Z to zero kills every block conjunction and the extra conjunction. Restoring any member of Z permits its entire block to fire. Thus Z is a minimal zero certificate and |I∩Z|=t. The circuit intersection bound in `overlap.md` gives the claimed optimum.

Each F_I also has size strictly above N−1 over the arbitrary binary basis. Pin every block except two to its settings in I. Keep the omitted and one selected variable in each of the two remaining blocks, setting their other bits to one. The resulting function is ab OR cd OR ac. The root bipartition argument in `recognition.md` excludes any binary read-once formula for it. Restriction preserves binary read-once representability. The exact excess t−1 above was proved for monotone circuits.

## The adversary and its constants

Couple the random tapes on F and on F_I. Let p≥2/3 be the probability of answering YES on F. Whenever I goes unqueried on a YES tape the algorithm also answers YES on F_I. Error at most 1/3 forces Pr[YES on F and I queried]≥p−1/3. Summing over I gives at least M/3 expected distinct queries on F. If the worst-case query cap is D, the same sum is at most pD, so D≥M/2. An exact deterministic algorithm needs all M on F. For this explicit family both randomized bounds are attained when M is even. With probability 1/3 answer NO immediately. Otherwise query a uniform subset of M/2 candidate inputs and answer NO exactly when a discrepancy is seen. For odd M rounding gives a worst-case cap of ⌈M/2⌉ and randomizing the subset size between its floor and ceiling attains the same expected bound.

The gate gap t−1 can be fixed in advance. Among these instances M is largest for balanced block sizes: if d_i exceeds d_j+1, moving one variable from i to j increases their product. Taking t proportional to εN for 0<ε≤1/4 yields exp(Ω(εN log(1/ε))) queries with a monotone optimum gap t−1. Taking d_i=3 when 3 divides N yields M=3^(N/3) and exact monotone sizes N−1 and 4N/3−2.

## Sharp monotone upper bound

First run the polynomial-time membership-query learner for monotone read-once formulas, with its promised-instance time bound. If it fails to return an all-essential monotone read-once formula in that time, reject. Otherwise let H be its hypothesis and check it on all its minterms and all its maximal false assignments. For an arbitrary monotone target, agreement at these points forces equality everywhere by passing down to a minterm or up to a maximal false input. True read-once inputs succeed by the learner's guarantee. [Angluin, Hellerstein and Karpinski, Learning Read-Once Formulas with Queries](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/1989/5897.html)

For a monotone read-once formula, the number of minterms is an arithmetic expression in N ones using + at OR and multiplication at AND. Maximal false assignments obey the dual recurrence. The largest integer obtained by such an expression is the largest product B(N) of positive integers summing to N. Products of optimal partitions concatenate. A sum of factors each at least two is bounded by their product; if one summand is one, adding one to a part of an optimal partition increases its product by at least one. This proves the upper bound inductively. Each positive integer part can itself be built by addition, proving equality. The optimal partition uses threes, replacing a 3+1 by 2+2, and has B(N)≤3^(N/3) (B(1)=1). Thus verification needs at most 2B(N) membership queries following polynomially many learning queries. Taking block sizes to be the parts in an optimal partition in the lower construction gives M=B(N) for N≥5. Together these prove the stated Θ bound.

The leading constants can also be matched. Write a,b for the two frontier counts of an N-input read-once formula. Inductively ab≤N B(N). At an AND root the product is a₁a₂(b₁+b₂); the two summands are bounded by N₁B(N₁)B(N₂) and N₂B(N₁)B(N₂), using the individual bounds as well as the induction hypotheses. Concatenating partitions gives B(N₁)B(N₂)≤B(N). The OR case is dual. Since a,b≤B(N), expanding (B(N)−a)(B(N)−b)≥0 gives a+b≤B(N)+N. The reference learner followed by all frontier queries therefore uses B(N)+poly(N), against a deterministic lower bound of B(N). For bounded error, reject immediately with probability 1/3. Otherwise run the learner and check a uniform half of its candidate frontier, rounding up if needed. A non-read-once target either fails at the learning stage or differs at a checked frontier point with probability at least 1/2 conditional on this branch. This gives worst-case cap B(N)/2+poly(N) and expected query cost B(N)/3+poly(N), each matching the corresponding lower bound up to an additive polynomial.

Classical polynomial-size checking tests for a read-once target can promise that all competitors are read-once. The lower family above includes read-twice competitors. Discrepancies have density 2^(−N), so this is exact identification at a size threshold, distinct from testing functions that are a constant distance from the property. [Chistikov, Checking Tests for Read-Once Functions over Arbitrary Bases](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0631)

## Arbitrary binary functions

For N≥3 start from parity P, of size N−1. Flip it just at a prescribed input I to obtain P_I. This requires at most 2N−1 arbitrary binary gates: compute parity, compute the conjunction specifying that single input (negations of literal signs are incorporated into incident binary truth tables), and XOR the two. Every input is essential since, for each coordinate, all but one of its edges in the Boolean cube retain their parity difference.

Negating input variables and, when necessary, the output reduces every split test to parity flipped at zero. In any read-once root bipartition A|B choose the side A with at least two variables. Nonzero A-assignments of even and odd parity give the ordinary parity function of B and its complement as row types. The zero A-assignment gives ordinary parity of B flipped at its zero input. This is distinct from both preceding rows, since B is nonempty. A binary formula root could have only two row types. Hence every P_I has size above N−1. Apply the same query adversary with M=2^N. Deterministic exact recognition needs the full table on parity and bounded-error algorithms need at least 2^(N−1) queries in the worst case. Reading the table gives the matching order of growth.

## Six queries certify that the threshold fails

For all-essential monotone targets on N≥3 inputs, the worst-case number of labeled rows in a perfect certificate for a NO answer is exactly six. Give the two named inputs and two background assignments supplied by the minterm/maxterm characterization in `recognition.md`. In the AND background check the 11,10,01 corners; in the OR background check the 00,10,01 corners. Monotonicity determines the omitted corner of each square. Every read-once target fails at least one check. Only O(N) classical witness bits are required.

For tightness use the majority function on three inputs. Promoting its false singleton {i} produces the read-once function x_i OR (x_j AND x_k) and changes exactly that one row. Demoting its true doubleton {i,j} produces x_k AND (x_i OR x_j), also changing exactly that one row. A perfect rejecting certificate for majority must therefore contain six distinct rows. Extending the function and all six competitors by OR of N−3 fresh variables preserves every singleton discrepancy at the all-zero padding and keeps every input essential. In contrast, on a designated YES instance the planted family forces exponentially many classical verifier queries, even if an untrusted prover supplies the optimal formula. Fix an honest strategy and coins distribution on the reference YES instance, reuse that strategy on each NO instance, and apply the same coupling bound.
