# Sparse sample formula minimization and the exact cost of a third read

A single labeled sample can encode maximum independent set in the price of allowing one extra occurrence of selected variables. All positive examples have Hamming weight two. All negative examples have weight two or three. Every coordinate occurs in at most six examples, and both labels occur equally often. The target function is the monotone two-DNF listing the positive examples.

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a nonempty simple cubic graph with \(n\) vertices and \(m\) edges. Its sample has \(n+2m\) coordinates and \(6m\) labeled examples. For any \(U\subseteq V\), allow three occurrences for each old coordinate in \(U\) and two for every other coordinate. For binary monotone AND/OR formulas, counting gates, the minimum consistent size is exactly
\[
                   4m+n-1-\alpha(G[U]).
\]
The same number is the minimum size for the full target function under those caps. Thus the all-read-two optimum is \(4m+n-1\); the read-three and unrestricted optima both equal \(4m+\tau(G)-1\). The entire size advantage of the third allowed occurrence is the maximum independent-set size of the specified induced graph. Each input coordinate is essential for every monotone completion, as witnessed by one positive and one negative example that force that coordinate.

The construction has a polynomial-time repair procedure. From a consistent unrestricted formula it constructs a formula for the whole target with no extra occurrences of any variable. From a formula with \(s\) gates making \(t\) sample errors it constructs a correct formula with at most \(s+2t\) gates. If the input formula meets the indicated caps, the repaired formula can meet them too. In particular, minimizing the empirical objective “gates plus twice the number of errors” gives exactly the same optimum as requiring perfect consistency.

## The sample

Replace each edge \(uv\) by the three-edge path \(u,p_{uv},q_{uv},v\), using private coordinates. Write \(H\) for the resulting graph. It has \(3m\) edges, girth at least nine, and every vertex has degree two or three. For each edge \(e=ab\) in \(H\), include a positive row with exactly \(a,b\) present, and a negative row with support
\[
                        I_e=(N_H(a)\cup N_H(b))\setminus\{a,b\}.
\]
This is independent. An edge among neighbors of one endpoint would make a triangle, and an edge between the two sides would make a four-cycle. A central edge gives the negative pair \(\{u,v\}\). An outer edge gives a negative triple of private coordinates. Every old coordinate has three positive occurrences and three negative occurrences. A private coordinate occurs in two positives and three negatives. All rows are distinct. For a triple from the edge between \(u\) and one of its tips, the other two tips of \(u\) are its unique pair adjacent to the same old vertex. Thus the triple determines \(u\) and the omitted tip. Every central negative row determines its base edge, and all positive rows determine \(H\).

For each \(v\) take two different incident edges \(e,f\). The only member of the positive row \(e\) absent from \(I_f\) is \(v\). Every monotone consistent formula must mention \(v\), whether or not it computes the intended full target.

## A decoder for an arbitrary candidate formula

For now take any finite triangle- and four-cycle-free graph of minimum degree at least two, with the same edge-indexed tests. Suppose \(\phi\) is consistent. On a positive edge row \(e\) and a negative row \(I_f\), traverse the syntax tree of \(\phi\). At an OR take the first child true on \(e\); at an AND take the first child false on \(I_f\). The two evaluations continue to disagree. The leaf is a variable in \(e\setminus I_f\). Each physical leaf occurrence receives a Cartesian product of row and column indices, because every OR decision depends only on the row and every AND decision only on the column.

Assign each edge \(e\) to the leaf label reached at \((e,I_e)\). This is an endpoint of \(e\). For a vertex \(v\), let \(d\) be its degree and \(k\) its number of assigned edges. Restrict the protocol grid to the \(d\) edges incident to \(v\) on both axes. At an off-diagonal position the unique possible output is \(v\). At a diagonal position the output is \(v\) exactly on its \(k\) assigned edges. The matrix of these positions is a sum of at most one rank-one rectangle indicator per occurrence of \(v\). If \(k>0\), its columns span the all-ones vector and \(d-k\) independent coordinate vectors, of joint rank \(1+d-k\). If \(k=0\), the matrix is \(J-I\), of rank \(d\) over the reals for \(d\ge2\). Consequently \(v\) has at least \(d-k+\mathbf1[k>0]\) occurrences.

Make one star at each used owner, with a disjunction of the other endpoints of its assigned edges, and OR the stars together. This computes the full edge predicate, since every edge is assigned exactly once. The number of \(v\)-occurrences is precisely \(d-k+\mathbf1[k>0]\). The construction uses \(|E(H)|+|T|-1\) gates, where \(T\) is the set of used owners. Formula traversal and rebuilding are polynomial-time operations. Only the diagonal traversals are needed to compute the orientation. The off-diagonal grid certifies its size bound. The edge-indexed rank tests originate in Jukna's graph-function lower bound. Here the diagonal is retained to identify and bound each owner's local cost. [Jukna, Boolean Function Complexity, §7.2](https://web.vu.lt/mif/s.jukna/boolean/bool-V7.pdf).

## Recovering the cover and respecting the read caps

Any cover \(T'\) of the subdivided graph selects at least one private tip on each base path, to cover the central edge. It selects both tips when it omits both old endpoints. Start with its old vertices and add one endpoint of each uncovered base edge. The resulting cover \(T\) of \(G\) has size at most \(|T'|-m\). If \(T'\) already contained some prescribed old vertices, so does \(T\).

For any cover \(T\) of \(G\), take all of \(T\) and one private tip per edge. If an old endpoint is omitted, use its adjacent tip. Where both endpoints are present either tip works. Assign each outer edge incident to an old selected vertex to that vertex, give the central edge to its chosen tip, and assign the remaining outer edges to their selected tips. The number of used centers is \(m+|T|\). Every old selected vertex occurs only once. An omitted old vertex occurs three times, and any private coordinate occurs at most twice.

An old vertex of read cap two must be a used owner in the orientation decoded from a consistent formula, since degree three and zero owned edges would require three occurrences. Thus the decoded \(T'\) contains \(V\setminus U\). The minimum size of a base cover containing these vertices is \(n-\alpha(G[U])\). The lower bound and matching construction give the claimed formula. The argument for unrestricted formulas uses the same orientation and every optimal star formula constructed here already meets read cap three.

## Error correction costs at most two gates per mislabeled example

Discard the \(b\) edges with an incorrect positive or paired negative. Write \(d\) for the number whose two examples are both wrong; \(t=b+d\). The diagonal decoder yields a set \(T_0\) of owners of the remaining edges. A local rank deficit can occur only if \(v\) has residual degree one and no occurrence anywhere in the supplied formula. Let \(e=va\) be its good edge. Then \(f(\{a\})=1\) since the positive row \(\{v,a\}\) is accepted and the formula ignores \(v\). For any other original incident edge \(j=vw\), its other endpoint \(w\) lies in the correctly rejected \(I_e\). Thus \(f(\{v,w\})=f(\{w\})=0\). Meanwhile \(a\in I_j\) gives \(f(I_j)=1\). Both labels on \(j\) are wrong. Every original degree is at least two, so each deficient vertex can be assigned to a doubly wrong edge; any one edge charges at most its two endpoints. The formula has at least \((3m-b)+|T_0|-2d\) variable leaves.

Assign the \(b\) removed edges to endpoints. At most \(b\) further owners appear. Converting to stars yields at most \(3m+|T_0|+b-1\le s+2b+2d=s+2t\) gates.

For the cap-preserving variant, whenever a removed outer edge has an old endpoint outside \(U\), choose that endpoint as its owner. Every old \(v\notin U\) with no good owned edge must have lost an incident edge, or its original degree-three submatrix would force three occurrences. Each removed edge touches at most one old vertex, so these simultaneous choices include every prescribed old vertex without increasing the number of newly added owners past \(b\). Convert the resulting cover to one of \(G\) containing \(V\setminus U\), then rebuild the full formula by the explicit one-tip-per-path construction above. Its size cannot increase and the output respects every cap. For \(t=0\), direct star conversion also gives occurrence-by-occurrence domination.

## Complexity and learning consequences

The size decision problem for explicit sparse samples is in NP. If a positive row is contained in a negative row no monotone function is consistent. Otherwise the OR of the positive rows' conjunctions has size linear in the sparse input, so a polynomial-size certificate exists whenever the threshold allows a solution. Constants cover the empty-label edge cases.

Minimum vertex cover is APX-complete on simple cubic graphs. The reduction above has optimum \(4m+\tau(G)-1\le13\tau(G)\), since each cover vertex covers at most three edges. From any consistent formula of size \(s\) it extracts a base cover of size at most \(s-4m+1\). Thus it is an L-reduction with constants 13 and 1, giving NP-hardness of the exact decision problem and APX-hardness of minimization on this sample family. The broader class with positive weight two, negative weight at most three, and at most six total appearances per variable is also in APX. Test all size-zero hypotheses. Otherwise a consistent nonconstant formula with \(s\ge1\) gates uses at most \(s+1\) different variables; every positive row contains at least one of them because the all-zero input is false. Hence there are at most \(6(s+1)\) positives. Their edge-DNF has at most \(12s+11\le23s\) gates. [Alimonti and Kann, Theorem 3.1 and its cubic regularization](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-3975(98)00158-3).

The repair inequality turns an additive gap in the optimum into a constant error fraction. More explicitly, let a cubic-cover gap instance distinguish \(\tau(G)\le K\) from \(\tau(G)\ge(1+\gamma)K\) for a fixed \(\gamma>0\), with \(K\ge m/3\) in the nontrivial range. Set \(s=4m+K-1\). In the yes case there is a consistent formula of size \(s\) or less. In the no case every formula of at most \(s\) gates makes at least \(\gamma K/2\ge\gamma m/6\) errors, a fraction at least \(\gamma/36\) of the uniform distribution on the sample. This is a proper bounded-size learning distinction for the explicit distribution.

There is an exact counting consequence. A set of permissions \(U\) realizes their entire potential saving of \(|U|\) gates if and only if \(U\) is independent. Thus the number of fully effective permission masks is the number of independent sets of \(G\), including the empty set, and the coefficient at size \(j\) counts its independent \(j\)-sets. The census on these promised samples is #P-complete under polynomial-time counting reductions: membership uses the adjacency test recovered from the positively labeled subdivided graph, and the reduction from cubic independent sets is one-to-one on masks. [Greenhill, The complexity of counting colourings and independent sets in sparse graphs and hypergraphs](https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~csg/papers/complexity.pdf).

The same fixed sample supports exact search with a variable read-budget oracle. A query on \(U\) returns \(\alpha(G[U])\) by subtracting the optimum from \(4m+n-1\); threshold decisions suffice by binary search. Querying whether the optimum changes upon deleting a vertex permits reconstructing a maximum independent set by the usual include/exclude self-reduction. Conversely one maximum-independent-set search on \(G[U]\) yields an optimal formula directly. Only the per-variable read caps vary between queries.

Ilango explicitly distinguishes an input/output pair list, whose partial circuit minimization problem was known to be NP-complete under deterministic many-one reductions, from a full length-\(2^n\) ternary truth table. His Theorem 3 gives an ETH lower bound in the latter representation, including a monotone read-once consistency consequence. [Ilango, Constant Depth Formula and Partial Function Versions of MCSP are Hard, §1, “Input Representation and Closeness,” ECCC TR20-183](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2020/183/).

Sparse sample formula minimization was already hard in other settings. For example, all-zero negative input and positive pairs already reduce vertex cover to choosing the fewest literals of a single OR, a restriction with many inessential inputs. The result here gives an explicit full target with every variable forced, pointwise repair, a noise bound, and an exact two-versus-three occurrence hierarchy. Cavalar, de Rezende, Gray and Santhanam prove stronger inapproximability against arbitrary monotone circuits for general samples under randomized ETH. Ilango proves ETH-hardness and search-to-decision results for minimum De Morgan formulas represented by complete truth tables. The sparse target and decoder in this note concern monotone formulas and explicit examples. No priority claim is made for unrestricted Occam learning hardness. [Cavalar et al., Theorem 1.2](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2026.40) [Ilango, The Minimum Formula Size Problem is (ETH) Hard](https://doi.org/10.1137/22M1481579).
