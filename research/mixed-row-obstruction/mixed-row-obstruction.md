# A rank-three row together with a sparse rank-four normal

Let \(P(M)=\sum_{\sigma:[4]\hookrightarrow[5]}\prod_i M_{i,\sigma(i)}\), and let \(J\) denote the all-ones matrix. Let \(L\subset\mathbb C^{4\times5}\) have dimension seven, satisfy \(P(J+X)=120\) for every \(X\in L\), and project injectively onto each pair of rows.

**Theorem.** Suppose one row projection has rank three, the other three have rank four, and exactly two of those three have normals of full support. The third rank-four normal has support of size at most two.

The proof below reduces the proposed supports three and four to 232 explicitly generated orientations. The [verifier](verify_mixed_rows.py) checks the elimination in every orientation.

## The two active sets

Call the rank-three row low. Its affine image is \(Ua=v=U\mathbf1\) with \(U\) a rank-two matrix and \(v\ne0\), since a zero row would make the permanent zero. Write \(p_j\) for its columns. Each rank-four translated row has normalization \(w\cdot b=1\). Its joint projection with the low row is the entire product because both the source and product have dimension seven.

Fix a full normal \(w\). The [rank-three two-column argument](rank-three-row.md) through its conclusion about the two distinct projective points uses only this one full normal. For clarity, when \(p_j,p_k\) are independent, the low row can be supported on \(j,k\) and must have both entries nonzero. If one entry were zero, the full row could occupy the same single column. Varying the full row supported on \(j,k\) makes the product of the two-row factor \(a_jb_k+a_kb_j\) and the remaining permanent a nonzero constant. Both factors are polynomials, hence constant. Consequently \(a_jw_j=a_kw_k\). Projecting \(p_j/w_j,p_k/w_k\) modulo \(\mathbb C v\) yields nonzero opposite scalars. Three distinct projective classes would give an odd cycle of opposites. Within each of the resulting two classes \(A,B\), the vectors \(p_j/w_j\) are constant. The affine constraints on the low row therefore prescribe the same nonzero value for \(\sum_A w_ja_j\) and \(\sum_B w_ja_j\). In particular \(\sum_A w_j=\sum_B w_j\ne0\).

The second full normal has constant ratio to the first on \(A\cup B\) by the same two-support test. The [full/full classification](full-row-obstruction.md) forbids global proportionality. Thus \(u=|A\cup B|\) is between two and four. Let \(D\) be the support of the sparse rank-four normal. If exactly one of a cross pair \(j\in A,k\in B\) were in \(D\), the sparse row supported on this pair would have an arbitrary coordinate and the two-row factor could not be constant. Thus \(D\) either contains all of \(A\cup B\), or is disjoint from it. In the first case the same variation shows the sparse/full normal ratio is constant on \(A\cup B\). If \(u=4\) and \(D\supseteq A\cup B\), a proper support is exactly that four-set. A common-ratio sparse/full pair requires two missing indices by the [sparse/full classification](sparse-high-pair.md), an impossibility. In the disjoint case a support of size at least three forces \(u=2, |D|=3\).

## Phases and necessary ranks

Normalize the three rank-four normals as \(v_i/s_i\), where \(v_{ij}=E_{ij}x_j\), \(s_i=\sum_j v_{ij}\). All five \(x_j\) and the three \(s_i\) are nonzero. The first full phase row is all ones, the second has fourth roots of unity and equals one on \(A\cup B\), and the sparse phase row has signs on \(D\) and zero elsewhere. Its first supported phase can be normalized to one. The low constraints use the first full row weights, so \(\sum_Ax_j=\sum_Bx_j\ne0\). Sort the inactive phase columns, and order the two low classes by size. This leaves the enumeration in the functions `cm`, `choices` and `iter_all`.

Every pair of rank-four rows gives the linear covector from the appropriate [full/full](full-row-obstruction.md) or [sparse/full](sparse-high-pair.md) classification. Test both orientations for a three-group full/full pair or a two-ratio sparse/full pair. For a common-ratio sparse/full pair enumerate all disjoint nonempty ordered \(P,Q\subseteq D^c\). If its sparse-to-full phase ratio on \(D\) is \(c\), and its sparse row is \(i\) and full row \(k\), its quadratic and physical tangent covector are
\[
 c s_k\sum_{j\in P} v_{kj} + s_i\sum_{j\in Q}v_{kj}-s_is_k=0,\qquad
 c s_k e_i\otimes v_k|_P+s_i e_k\otimes v_k|_Q .
\]
Keeping \(c\) allows nonreal relative phases when \(D\) is disjoint from the two low classes.

Rescaling coordinates by weights and row totals leaves eight linear constraints independent of weights: three rank-four normals, the two low block normals, and the three pair relations. For each two-row projection there are three independent supported constraints, and the kernel of the eight displayed rows must contain the hypothesized seven-dimensional image. Its projected codimension must therefore equal three. The function `passes` checks this using the [exact Gaussian-integer rank test](sparse-row-obstruction.md). This leaves 98 containing and 62 disjoint orientations of support three, and 72 containing orientations of support four.

For the physical tangent test, let \(F\) contain those eight weight-dependent covectors and the total-sum covector. As in the [full-row proof](full-row-obstruction.md),
\[
 K=(3I_4-\mathbf1\mathbf1^t)\otimes(4I_5-\mathbf1\mathbf1^t),
 \qquad \operatorname{rank}(F K F^t)\le6 .
\]
This rank bound follows because the row space of \(F\) lies in the annihilator of a seven-dimensional totally isotropic subspace of the nondegenerate quadratic form.

## Finite cases

In the containing case \(A\) is a singleton. For \(u=2\) its mass equals the mass of the other singleton. For \(u=3\) it equals the sum of the two masses in \(B\), whose individual values do not enter the pair quadratics. There are \(6-u\) aggregate variables. In the disjoint case eliminate one of the two equal low masses to obtain four variables. For each orientation the prover uses exact degree-bounded Macaulay kernels, contraction, and preservation of the common evaluation covector, as developed in the [full-row proof](full-row-obstruction.md). Every individual conclusion follows from the verified matrices. The number of quadratics only chooses which algorithm to apply.

In a doubled block \(B\), restore its two masses as \(\eta+t,\eta-t\), with singleton mass \(2\eta\) and the appropriate common scaling of outside masses. Swapping these two coordinates fixes every Gram entry. Its coefficient of \(t^2\) factors through four row coordinates per operator dimension \(b\). Consequently the projected determinant degree in \(t^2\) is at most \(4b\). An attained degree and coprime projected determinants at a split prime exclude a common complex root by the [degree-preserving specialization argument](full-row-obstruction.md).

For support three, 29 open loci are empty and 90 fail the Gram test. For support four, 48 fail the Gram test. In the remaining 41 and 24 orientations, respectively, \(u=2\), the full/full pair has two sign groups, and all phases are real signs or zero.

## Curves and pencils

Scale so that the signed sparse total equals one. For a two-ratio sparse/full pair let \(y\) be the signed full total on \(D\), \(t\) the signed missing total, and \(h=\pm1\) the phase ratio on the first chosen group. Then \(z=hy-1\) satisfies \(z^2+htz=2\). This identity and the descriptions below use ordinary complex bilinear forms.

* **Both sparse/full pairs have two ratios.** There are 24 containing orientations of support four and 12 disjoint orientations of support three. In the former, combine the equal low masses into one supported mass and let \(t\) be the one missing mass. In the latter, the two equal low masses are the only missing coordinates and \(t\) is their sum. The two full rows see missing totals \(e_i t\) with \(e_i=\pm1\). The roots of \(v^2+tv=2\) are \(v,-2/v\), and \(t=2/v-v\). Each full active total is \(h_i+e_i v_i\) for one of those roots. The three-by-three signed matrix recovering the three effective supported masses is invertible in every orientation, checked by its adjugate. Choosing the first root as parameter and both choices for the second covers all points. Of the resulting 72 parametrizations, 18 lack an essential nonzero weight or sum identically and 54 fail the exact univariate Gram test.
* **Containing support three, one common ratio.** In 16 orientations the two missing coordinates give singleton groups \(P,Q\) for this pair. Their signed weights are \(t,1/t-t\) since \(t(t+q)=1\). Up to sign, their sum for the other pair is either \(1/t\) or \(2t-1/t\). For the former, use \(v^2+v/t=2\), giving \(t=v/(2-v^2)\). For the latter the two roots are \(1/t,-2t\). If \(y\) is the signed active full total, the equal low weights are \((1+y)/4\) and the remaining supported weight is \(E_{2d}(1-y)/2\), since the full phases agree on the low coordinates and have opposite relative phase on \(d\). Of the resulting 24 parametrizations, four lose an essential nonzero factor and 20 fail the univariate Gram test.
* **Disjoint support three, at least one common ratio.** There are eight orientations with one such pair and two with both. The two missing low weights equal \(p\), with unit full phase on both. A common-ratio full row has signed active total \(c\), \(c=\pm1\). Its equation is \(c(c+2p)p+p-(c+2p)=c(2p^2-1)=0\), so \(p^2=1/2\). If the second pair is two-ratio, adjoin \(z\) with \(z^2+2hpz=2\). The two signed sparse group totals are \((1\pm hy)/2\). Within the repeated group, the two columns have identical phases, so splitting evenly plus and minus a scalar gives all weights. If both pairs are common-ratio, the three supported columns have the same phases. The Gram depends only on their total and their sum of squares: its entries use ordinary coordinatewise pairings and products of sums. For fixed total, the family of equal baseline weights plus \((v,-v,0)\) realizes every sum of squares over \(\mathbb C\). Use the two unit low covectors on their singleton coordinates. The derivative from the split uses only the three high row slots in a column-difference direction. The row metric there is \(3I_3-\mathbf1\mathbf1^t\), of rank two. The projected determinant therefore has degree at most \(2b\) in \(v^2\), where \(b\) is the dimension of the representation of the two monic quadratics. In the case with no \(z\), just the quadratic for \(p\) is used. The attained degree and coprime reductions exclude all ten pencils.
* **Containing support three, both common-ratio.** For the remaining three orientations substitute signed weights \(t,1/t-t\) from the first common-ratio pair into the second. After exact removal of the nonzero coordinate factors its numerator is constant in one case and has square-free part \(2t^2-1\) in the other two. In these two, the signed missing weights coincide and the signed third supported phase is one. The supported weights are \((v,v,1-2v)\). Take unit low covectors again. Their fixed support and the constant row totals make \(F\) affine in \(v\). Its linear coefficient is supported in the three high rows, on the single column vector \((1,1,-2)\) on those three columns. Consequently it has rank at most \(3b\). Left and right variation in a projected Gram determinant give degree at most \(6b\) in \(v\). The verifier attains this degree and obtains coprime reductions in both cases.

For the rational curves, the verifier clears common denominators and computes integer determinant polynomials by interpolation with the homogeneous degree bounds in the [sparse-row proof](sparse-row-obstruction.md). Every denominator divided out in the parametrization is nonzero at a genuine candidate. It removes factors of the specified nonzero forms by exact division and takes a primitive polynomial gcd. The finite-pencil tests use the split-prime degree criterion described above. These cases account for every retained orientation, proving the stated support bound.
