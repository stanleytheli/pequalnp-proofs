# The exceptional rank-three row has a fixed coordinate

Keep \(P,J,L\) as in the full-row obstruction. Suppose no nonzero member of the seven-plane \(L\) is supported on at most two rows. The projection onto every pair of rows is then injective, so individual row ranks are at least three and at most one row can have rank three.

Assume one row, called row zero, has rank three, and the three other rows have rank four with full-support normals. Then some coordinate of row zero is identically zero on \(L\). In fact, after permuting columns its tangent row space either has two zero coordinates and three free coordinates, or has a single zero coordinate, two nonzero coordinates with a weighted sum zero, and two free coordinates. In the latter case the weights on the two active coordinates are nonzero.

To prove this, write the affine image of the translated row zero as \(\{a:Ua=v\}\), where \(U\) has rank two and \(v=U\mathbf1\ne0\). Write \(p_j\) for its columns and call the indices where \(p_j\ne0\) active. Pick any of the three full rows, with normalized full-support normal \(w\), so its translated image is \(w\cdot b=1\). The row-pair projection has dimension seven, the sum of the individual ranks, hence its image is the entire product.

Whenever \(p_j,p_k\) are independent there is a unique row \(a\) supported on \(\{j,k\}\) with \(p_j a_j+p_k a_k=v\). Both of those entries are nonzero. Otherwise the other full row could also be concentrated at the sole active coordinate and the full permanent would be zero. On that same column pair the full row varies along its one-dimensional weighted normalization. The full permanent factors as \(a_j b_k+a_k b_j\) times the two-row permanent on the three other columns. The second factor is polynomial on the line because the pair projection is an affine isomorphism. Thus \(a_jb_k+a_kb_j\) is constant and nonzero, giving \(a_j w_j=a_k w_k\). Consequently \(p_j/w_j+p_k/w_k\) lies along \(v\).

Every nonzero \(p_j\) lies in some independent pair. Its projection modulo \(\mathbb C v\) is nonzero because \(v\) cannot be represented by a single \(p_j\). For every independent pair their projected scalars \((p_j/w_j)\bmod\mathbb C v\) are opposite. If the active columns of \(U\) represented three distinct projective points, this would produce a triangle of nonzero mutually opposite scalars. Hence there are exactly two distinct projective points, defining nonempty classes \(A,B\). Within each class \(p_j/w_j\) is constant by comparison modulo \(v\) with the other class and proportionality in the same class. The two constants are independent. The affine row-zero constraints therefore prescribe separately \(\sum_Aw_ja_j\) and \(\sum_Bw_ja_j\). These prescribed values agree and are nonzero by the two-support test above.

For every full row normal \(w'\), the same test gives \(w'_j/w_j=w'_k/w_k\) on every cross pair \(j\in A,k\in B\). Therefore all three full rows' normals are proportional on the full active set \(A\cup B\). At least two columns outside it are necessary. If none stood outside, the normals would be proportional globally, contrary to the two-row ratio proposition. With just one outside column, normalize all three normals to agree on the active set. Each pair would then have exactly two ratio values, and the value on the one outside coordinate would have to be \(-1\) for every pair. Three nonzero numbers cannot have all pairwise ratios equal to \(-1\). Thus \(2\le |A\cup B|\le3\). Each inactive column is free, and at least one of the two weighted blocks is a singleton. This proves the assertion.
