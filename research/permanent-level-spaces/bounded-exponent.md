# A permanent bound for regular abelian row actions of bounded exponent

Let a finite abelian group G of cardinality m act regularly on the rows of an m by q complex matrix, where q≤m. Write J for the all-ones matrix and P for the rectangular permanent, summing one product for every assignment of the q columns to distinct rows. If a complex linear subspace L is invariant under row translations and P(J+T)=P(J) throughout L, its dimension is at most (m−1)(e−1) whenever the positive integer e annihilates G. In particular an abelian family of bounded exponent has a linear bound in its number of rows, even when several primes divide its order. The full result with the usual rectangular permanent is BoundedFrequency.bounded_exponent_translated.

For one character χ of image order d, its matrices are T(g,j)=χ(g)v(j). Let Vχ consist of the vectors v giving matrices in L. The degree-k coefficient of P(J+tT) is a positive combinatorial factor times the k-th elementary symmetric functions of the row character values and the column vector. The character values consist of a full set of d-th roots of unity, each repeated m/d times. Their characteristic polynomial is a power of Xᵈ−1. Every coefficient in degrees divisible by d is nonzero. Thus every v in Vχ satisfies eₖ(v)=0 whenever d divides k and 0<k≤q.

For a primitive d-th root z, the average over i of ∏ⱼ(1+zⁱvⱼ) is 1. This follows by expanding and summing powers of z. If Vχ had dimension at least d, there would be d distinct coordinate projections on which it surjects. Prescribe the values −z⁻ⁱ in those coordinates. Every product in the average would now contain a zero factor, a contradiction. Hence dim Vχ≤d−1. In particular the trivial character contributes no dimension.

Each T in L is the sum of its Fourier components, and each component belongs to L because it is a linear combination of row translates. There are m−1 nontrivial characters. Each has order at most e, proving the bound.

Under the additional hypothesis of independently transitive column permutations, square matrices satisfy a stronger conclusion. For any nonzero Fourier component, its column-vector space is transitive on its coordinates. Every coordinate is therefore nonzero on some vector. A finite union of proper hyperplanes cannot cover a complex vector space, so a single vector exists that is nonzero in every coordinate. Its rank-one character matrix has a nonzero top-degree square permanent: the top coefficient is m! times the product of the row character values and the product of the column entries. This contradicts constancy. Consequently the two-sided space is zero. The column action can be nonabelian.
