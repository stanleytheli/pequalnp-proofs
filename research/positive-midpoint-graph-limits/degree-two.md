# The exact locality threshold at maximum degree two

Consider the same real positive limits, with finite values at the Boolean inputs and bounded value at the cube midpoint. Suppose H has maximum degree two. If each vertex probability depends on at most d bits, then the Boolean support has a CNF of width at most 3d, with at most 1+N+binom(N,2)+binom(N,3) clauses. Conversely every CNF of width at most 3d can be represented using O(1) vertices per clause at fixed girth. The component graphs can be odd cycles of any prescribed odd length at least nine. At polynomial graph order this precisely characterizes the possible supports. On bipartite graphs the corresponding width is 2d. The general maximum-degree-three, positive coefficientwise construction can already represent NP/poly supports on graphs of growing girth.

## Three incompatible pins suffice

Use a good cover to mean a cover whose rescaled midpoint weight has strictly positive limit on the common subsequence. A pin means that a specified vertex must be omitted from that cover, equivalently that it must be selected in the complementary independent set. Good covers in distinct components can be recombined independently. Indeed, swapping an entire component between two covers gives two legal covers with the same product of weights as before. If both original limiting weights are positive, the two recombined weights are positive. Iterating component swaps shows that a set of requested pins is feasible whenever its demands are feasible separately on each component. An empty good family gives the identically false condition, which is described by one empty clause.

A connected graph of maximum degree two is an isolated vertex, path, or cycle. Paths and even cycles have the two-pin property from the bipartite theorem. On an odd cycle, consider a minimal infeasible set S of pins. If a pair is adjacent, that pair is already infeasible. Otherwise choose v∈S. If v alone is infeasible, minimality settles the case. Condition on v being selected. Both its neighbors are necessarily in the cover, and the remainder after deleting v and its neighbors is a path or empty. In this slice, good covers again form a bipartite lattice on the remainder: hold the three fixed statuses constant while applying the two recombinations, and use the product equality at finite stages and in the limit. Good covers may be conditioned after choosing a component, since a component restriction of a good cover can be completed by any previously fixed good choices elsewhere. If more than two further pins are requested, infeasibility already occurs for one or two of them in the slice. Adding v gives an infeasible subset of size at most three. Minimality proves |S|≤3. More generally this same proof applies whenever deleting a pinned vertex and all its neighbors leaves a bipartite graph.

For every infeasible set S of at most three vertices require the clause OR_{v∈S} D_v, where D_v=[d_v(x)>0] is the single disjunction from the midpoint normalization. This gives the bounds on count and width. Erasing impossible pairs inside a single graph edge is included in this rule.

## Odd-cycle realization of one clause

Place three marked vertices of an odd cycle of length L≥9 at clockwise gaps 3,3,L−6. Give each vertex absence probability t, except for marked vertex i where it is t·dᵢ(x). Each dᵢ is the average of any assigned group of input literals and is zero for an empty group. The p_v=1−q_v are affine and cube preserving. Multiplication by t^−⌈L/2⌉ has a finite coefficientwise limit: it sums the absent-vertex factors of all minimum covers, equivalently all maximum independent sets.

For independent prescribed vertices on a cycle at circular gaps g₁,…,g_k, the maximum size of an independent extension is Σ⌊gᵢ/2⌋. In a gap of length g≥2, the two endpoint neighbors are forbidden and the interior path allows ⌊g/2⌋−1 additional vertices. For one pin use the same formula by cutting the cycle there; with no pins take its usual maximum independent set. On an odd cycle any pair of nonadjacent marked vertices has one even and one odd gap, hence extends to size ⌊L/2⌋. With all three marked vertices pinned there are three odd gaps, and the largest extension is one smaller. At a Boolean input a marked vertex is forced exactly when dᵢ(x)=0. Thus the limit is positive exactly when d₁(x)>0 OR d₂(x)>0 OR d₃(x)>0. Assign up to d input literals to each group. Disjoint union of cycles and multiplication of their normalizations realizes a CNF with one such component per clause. These are genuine coefficientwise polynomial limits, including at the cube midpoint.

## Tight graph size for parity

The 2^(n−1) clause lower bound for odd parity gives N=Ω(2^(n/3)) when degrees are at most two and affine parameters can use arbitrarily many inputs. The following upper bound has the same order.

Partition the input into three nonempty blocks of sizes differing by at most one. For each of the four forbidden even-total triples of block parities, build a separate odd cycle. Use three consecutive groups of marked positions. In group j put one vertex for every assignment of block j having that triple's prescribed local parity. Choose all first-group positions at even indices, all second-group positions at odd indices, and all third-group positions at even indices. Leave at least three steps between groups and around the wrap, and choose an odd cycle length. Constant spacing and padding cost O(2^⌈n/3⌉) vertices per cycle. At a marked vertex use t times the normalized Hamming distance of the associated block from the stored assignment as its absence probability. At every other vertex use t.

Each group has at most one zero-distance pin, and has one exactly if its block parity matches. When all three match, the gaps from group one to group two, from two to three and across the wrap are odd. Its minimum-cover contribution vanishes. When at most two match, those vertices extend to a maximum independent set. The four disjoint cycles therefore exclude precisely the four even-total patterns, with a positive coefficientwise limit everywhere else on the Boolean cube. This proves Θ(2^(n/3)) graph order. The small block-degenerate cases n≤2 can be represented separately.

The bipartite rate Θ(2^(n/2)) from the other note is attainable even on a disjoint union of two paths. For each of the forbidden block-parity pairs use an even-order path, with all first-block record markers at even one-based positions, and all later second-block markers at odd one-based positions. Each zero-distance record forces its vertex into a maximum independent set. On a path with 2h vertices the size-h independent sets are exactly an odd-position prefix followed by an even-position suffix, with one selected vertex per consecutive pair (1,2), (3,4), … . An even-position marker followed by an odd-position one is thus incompatible; either alone is feasible. Use the same t-normalized absence probabilities and integrate over maximum independent sets. The path lengths total O(2^⌈n/2⌉). The two components exclude precisely the two even-total block-parity pairs. All the degree thresholds here concern the physical graph, irrespective of how many coordinates a given affine parameter reads.
