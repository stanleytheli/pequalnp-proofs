# A midpoint bound for positive limits on bipartite graphs

Let H be a finite bipartite graph with sides L,R, N vertices in total, and let Q_H(p) be the probability that the independently selected vertices, with probabilities p_v, contain no edge. At stage j give each vertex a real affine function p_{v,j}(x) mapping [0,1]ⁿ into [0,1]. A positive number c_j may rescale the output. Suppose c_j Q_H(p_j(x)) converges to a finite value f(x) at every Boolean x, and stays bounded at the single additional point z=(1/2,…,1/2). The support {x∈{0,1}ⁿ : f(x)>0} has a CNF with at most N+|L||R| clauses when it is nonempty. The empty support needs only the empty clause. If every affine parameter depends on at most d coordinates, clause width is at most 2d. The scales c_j can be unbounded, the affine coefficients can change signs, and no power-series or smooth dependence on j is required. A coefficientwise polynomial limit satisfies the midpoint hypothesis. A bounded graph order at fixed n also allows the graphs to vary with j, by passing to a constant-graph subsequence.

## Taking one common subsequence

Put q_{v,j}=1−p_{v,j} and a_{v,j}=q_{v,j}(z). If a_{v,j}>0, put d_{v,j}=q_{v,j}/a_{v,j}; otherwise put d_{v,j}=0. Nonnegative affine functions on the cube satisfy q(x)+q(1−x)=2q(z). Thus 0≤d_{v,j}≤2 throughout the cube, including when a_{v,j}=0 (then q vanishes identically). The coefficients of these affine forms are bounded by comparing their values at zero and the unit vectors. Pass to a common subsequence along which all their coefficients converge to nonnegative affine forms d_v. We mean that each *form* is nonnegative on the cube; its individual linear coefficients can have either sign. Under a locality bound d, pass first to a constant coordinate support for each vertex.

The event counted by Q is the union over vertex covers C of the event that every vertex in C is absent. Indeed the complement of any independent selected set is itself a vertex cover. Each event has probability ∏_{v∈C}q_{v,j}(x). Define w_{C,j}=c_j∏_{v∈C}a_{v,j}. At the midpoint w_{C,j}≤c_j Q_H(p_j(z)). The finite set of covers therefore admits a further common subsequence on which w_{C,j}→w_C≥0 for every C. A cover is called good when w_C>0. For each x and C the rescaled event probability tends to w_C∏_{v∈C}d_v(x). Bounding a finite union below by its largest event and above by the sum of its events yields the equivalence

    f(x)>0  ⇔  there is a good cover C with d_v(x)>0 for every v in C.

No independence between cover events is assumed. Positive support somewhere implies existence of a good cover. A good cover may have no satisfying Boolean point if its limiting forms require incompatible literals; the CNF derivation allows that case as well.

## Two incompatible vertices suffice

Encode a cover C by the two sets of vertices it omits. With this convention the pair operations (A_L∪B_L,A_R∩B_R) and (A_L∩B_L,A_R∪B_R) preserve the cover condition: a pair of omitted endpoints cannot span an edge. For these two recombined covers C′,D′ one has w_{C,j}w_{D,j}=w_{C′,j}w_{D′,j}, since the vertex multiplicities on the two sides agree and each side contains c_j². Take limits. If C,D are good then so are C′ and D′. Thus the feasible omitted sets form a finite lattice under these operations.

For a Boolean input, demand omission of each v with d_v(x)=0. Such demands are simultaneously feasible whenever each single demand is feasible and every demanded left–right pair is feasible. For a fixed right vertex r, take witnesses omitting it together with each of the requested left vertices and use the first operation to combine them. This gives one cover omitting r and all requested lefts. Then combine these covers for all requested r by the second operation. The singleton and nonempty-family assumptions handle empty sides. This is also the two-coordinate consistency property of a Boolean relation closed under coordinatewise majority.

Any nonnegative affine function d on the cube can be written as its minimum plus a sum of nonnegative multiples of literals x_i or 1−x_i, by collecting positive and negative coefficients separately. Therefore D_v(x)=[d_v(x)>0] is either a constant or a single disjunction of literals. For each vertex v which cannot be omitted by any good cover require D_v. For each opposite-side pair u,v which cannot be omitted together require D_u OR D_v. Each is a clause, and the preceding equivalence proves that their conjunction describes exactly the Boolean support. This gives N+|L||R| and the locality bound. This proof also applies to an arbitrary finite family of allowed omissions closed under the two operations.

## Converse and scope

For an N-vertex limit one can remove the limiting operation while keeping at most N+2 vertices and the same input locality. Retain each original vertex, with absence probability d_v/2, and introduce one always-present anchor on each side. Join each unomittable vertex to the opposite anchor. Join every opposite pair that cannot be omitted together. An ordinary no-edge probability on this bipartite graph is positive precisely when its always-present vertices at that input form an independent set: all other vertices have positive probability of being absent. This tests exactly the singleton and pair clauses. For the identically zero support use one always-present edge. The new graph can have higher degrees and smaller girth.

Every CNF with M clauses can occur as the Boolean support of an unscaled bipartite no-edge probability. For a clause K of positive width give one vertex the absence probability equal to the average of its literals, and force its partner on a private edge to be present. Its no-edge probability equals that average; disjoint edges multiply. The result is positive exactly when the CNF is true. Split a clause's literals across both endpoints if a locality budget d is imposed: its no-edge probability q_u+q_v−q_u q_v is positive iff either endpoint's average is positive. Handle an empty group by q=0. Thus, for polynomial graph order and fixed locality d, the characterized class is precisely polynomial-size CNF with width at most 2d. This is a characterization of Boolean supports; an arbitrary CNF is not claimed to have its exact multilinear extension represented by this particular product.

The midpoint condition is essential. With only corner convergence, an unbounded rescaling can hide an exponentially large CNF behind a linear-size bipartite graph. The companion nonreachability construction gives an explicit example. General graphs with odd cycles can, in contrast, realize positive coefficientwise witness-count limits for arbitrary NP/poly languages through a record construction. The assumptions that probabilities are cube-preserving and output scales positive matter throughout. The signed affine arithmetic projection for long bipartite paths uses field constants beyond this range.
