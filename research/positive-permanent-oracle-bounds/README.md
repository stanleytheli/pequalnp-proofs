# A positive affine border of the permanent cannot approximate Hamiltonian cycles at polynomial order

Consider k×k matrices whose entries are arbitrary real affine functions of the input variables x, each nonnegative on the entire unit cube. Negative linear coefficients are allowed. A sequence of such matrices A_j may change every entry and may be multiplied by arbitrary positive output scales c_j. Assume the outputs c_j per(A_j) stay bounded at just the center of the cube. Every coefficientwise limit along a subsequence has the following property: **its lowest nonzero homogeneous part is exactly a monotone affine projection of the permanent of the same order k**. In the resulting matrix, each entry is zero, a nonnegative constant or a homogeneous linear form with nonnegative coefficients. One may keep the original set of input coordinates at each matrix entry. In particular, for a homogeneous target the whole limiting polynomial has such a representation.

As a consequence, uniformly approximating the m-city Hamiltonian-cycle polynomial within any positive finite pair of multiplicative factors on a neighborhood of zero inside the nonnegative cube requires k≥2^{Ω(m)}. The factors may depend on m. It suffices to satisfy the bounds in pointwise liminf and limsup along the approximants. The upper bound at any one strictly interior input already bounds the midpoint. This statement concerns the numerical polynomial near the origin, not merely its Boolean support. The proof is unconditional and does not use the recently announced arithmetic separation.

Grochow proved an exponential lower bound for ordinary monotone affine projections from the permanent to Hamiltonian cycles using extension complexity. Here the input entries need only be nonnegative *as functions on the cube*. The sequence and its possibly unbounded rescaling permit degenerations. Extracting the initial polynomial gives an ordinary monotone projection to which the polyhedral obstruction applies. A priority claim for this extension has not been established. [Grochow, Theorems 3.1 and 4.2](https://toc.cs.uchicago.edu/articles/v013a018/v013a018.pdf) [Rothvoß, Corollary 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.2369)

## Normalize at the center

Write z=(1/2,…,1/2) and a_{e,j}=A_{e,j}(z). An affine form nonnegative on the cube satisfies A(x)+A(1−x)=2A(z). Thus a zero midpoint makes the form identically zero, and otherwise its normalized version is between zero and two throughout the cube. Fix a subsequence on which the set of identically zero entries is constant. Ratios D_{e,j}=A_{e,j}/a_{e,j} for every other entry have a common coefficientwise-convergent subsequence by bounding the values at zero and the coordinate unit vectors. Their limits D_e are nonnegative affine functions with D_e(z)=1.

For each permutation M avoiding the zero entries let w_{M,j}=c_j ∏_{e∈M}a_{e,j}. This is nonnegative and bounded above by the total scaled permanent at the center. Extract simultaneously finite limits w_M for them all. Then the limiting polynomial is

    F(x)=Σ_{M good} w_M ∏_{e∈M}D_e(x),    good means w_M>0.

The expansion also proves coefficientwise precompactness directly from the midpoint bound. The zero polynomial requires no further argument. A finite corner limit by itself need not identify the polynomial in the interior.

A bound at any one strictly interior point y suffices in place of the midpoint. Let λ be the minimum over all its coordinate distances from zero and one. For any x in the cube, y=λx+(1−λ)w for some w in the cube. Nonnegative affineness gives A(x)≤A(y)/λ at every entry, so the nonnegative scaled permanent at the midpoint is at most λ^{−k} times its value at y.

## Reconstruct one matching face and its amplitudes

Let E* be the union of the edges of all good permutations. Any perfect matching M using only E* is good. Take a finite list of good permutations whose multiset union contains every edge of M. Removing the edges of M from this regular bipartite multigraph leaves a regular bipartite multigraph of degree one less. It decomposes into that many perfect matchings by repeated use of Hall’s theorem. The product of the scaled midpoint weights of the original list equals the product for M and this decomposition at every stage, since edge multiplicities and powers of c_j agree. The left side has positive limit and all factors on the right have finite limits. Hence the factor w_M is positive. The argument includes a list of length one, with an empty residual product.

All good amplitudes factor over edges with one common positive scale. Indeed form the matrix whose row at a good M lists a 1 for the common scale and the zero/one incidence vector of M. On the sequence of logarithms of c_j and of the positive a_{e,j} its row values converge to log w_M. The column space is finite dimensional and closed. Hence these limiting logs are themselves row values at some fixed real vector. Exponentiating yields w_M=B∏_{e∈M}b_e with B,b_e>0. The factor B can be absorbed in every entry of a single row. Together these two steps show that the *entire* nonzero limit is already exactly a cube-nonnegative affine projection of the same k-permanent. Intermediate finite matrices need not remain entrywise bounded; the reconstructed fixed affine matrix is finite. If a uniform upper bound of one is required on each entry, divide all entries by a sufficiently large common constant and compensate with one output scale.

## Take the lowest homogeneous degree

Discard all edges outside E*. A remaining D_e either has positive value at zero, in which case its leading homogeneous part L_e is this positive constant, or vanishes there. In the latter case cube nonnegativity forces all its slopes to be nonnegative, by evaluation on each unit vector; its nonzero leading form L_e is linear. Give these edges costs zero and one respectively. Products of the L_e have positive value on the strict positive orthant, so there is no cancellation among minimum-cost matching terms. The lowest degree is exactly their common total cost.

The union of the minimum-cost perfect matchings contains only minimum-cost perfect matchings in the same sense as before: remove any supported perfect matching from a multiset of minima and decompose the remainder. The sum of costs stays equal to the number of matchings times the minimum, so every matching in the new decomposition also attains it. Keep only the edges in this union and replace each D_e by L_e. The permanent of the resulting nonnegative matrix (including the previously obtained factors) is the lowest homogeneous part F_d. Locality is preserved because every surviving nonconstant L_e uses only variables from that entry along a further subsequence with constant allowed coordinate sets.

## Multiplicative approximation and the Newton polytope

Suppose c HC_m(x)≤F(x)≤C HC_m(x) for all strictly positive x sufficiently near zero, with 0<c≤C<∞. For any strictly positive real vector v, substitute x=t v and let t decrease to zero. The leading component F_d(v) is strictly positive. Homogeneity of HC_m forces d=m, and after dividing by t^m the same inequalities hold with F_m(v) on the entire positive orthant. For arbitrary real u evaluate at v_i=exp(Tu_i). Dividing the log polynomial values by T and sending T to infinity gives the maximum dot product of u with a monomial exponent, since both polynomials have positive coefficients on their supports. The bounded multiplicative ratio therefore implies equal support functions and identical Newton polytopes. The proof works for any nonzero homogeneous target having nonnegative coefficients.

For completeness, the Newton polytope of a monotone affine k-permanent in n variables has an extension with O(k²(n+1)) inequalities. Start with the bipartite perfect-matching assignment constraints, forbidding missing edges. For each allowed edge e, split its matching variable y_e among nonnegative choices z_{e,i} summing to y_e, one choice per positive coefficient in its linear form and a zero-exponent choice if it has a constant term. Output the sum of z_{e,i} over edges for each coordinate i. Every permissible matching with one chosen monomial at each edge is represented. Conversely decompose a fractional assignment into perfect matchings; conditional on using edge e, choose the monomial i with probability z_{e,i}/y_e, ignoring zero y_e. The resulting expected exponent is the proposed output. Thus this extension is exact. With at most d input variables at each entry the count is O(k²(d+1)).

The directed tour polytope projects onto the ordinary undirected tour polytope by summing opposite arc coordinates. Rothvoß's exponential lower bound on the undirected tour polytope therefore applies to the Newton polytope of directed HC_m. Combining it with the displayed upper bound, where n=m², gives k≥2^{Ω(m)}. If approximating sequences satisfy only pointwise limiting upper and lower inequalities near the origin, first use midpoint precompactness to take a coefficientwise subsequence; its polynomial satisfies those same inequalities simultaneously. This gives the stated sequential version. The same proof applies to the generic perfect-matching polynomial of the complete undirected graph on even m vertices (the unsigned Pfaffian, or hafnian). Its Newton polytope is the perfect-matching polytope to which Rothvoß’s lower bound applies directly. Grochow’s Theorem 4.4 treats the corresponding ordinary monotone projection. His Open Question 5.2 asks about agreement on Boolean corners, which this neighborhood argument leaves open.

All arguments in this note are written proofs. The accompanying small-instance controls test the two matching-union arguments and the recovery of the lowest polynomial with signed slopes at nonzero intercepts. The note and controls do not certify the independent Anthropic proof.
