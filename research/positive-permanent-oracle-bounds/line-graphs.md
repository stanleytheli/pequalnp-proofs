# A graph family that represents the permanent and resists Hamiltonian approximation

An explicit family of subcubic claw-free perfect graphs on Θ(m²) vertices admits a positive coefficientwise affine degeneration of its independent-set probability to the m×m permanent. Every input appears just once. In the whole class of line graphs of bipartite graphs, allowing arbitrary cube-preserving affine entries, positive scales and coefficientwise limits, a polynomial-size family cannot even approximate the m-city Hamiltonian polynomial within a fixed multiplicative factor in a positive neighborhood of zero. These two conclusions concern the numerical polynomials. They give an unconditional separation between two classical counting polynomials in a restricted model; both the underlying graph and the locality conditions are specified below.

## Making the permanent with disjoint triangles

Start with the complete bipartite graph between m row vertices and m column vertices. Replace each old vertex of degree m by a path on 2m−1 vertices, alternating m ports and m−1 private fillers. Give each old incidence its own distinct port. Keep each old edge, now connecting the assigned ports. Row blocks and column blocks use opposite colors for their ports, so the graph is bipartite. It is simple, subcubic, and has O(m²) vertices and edges.

In any perfect matching exactly one external edge leaves each port block: each of the m−1 fillers must use a different port, leaving just one port for an external edge. Conversely, choosing one external edge at each old vertex extends uniquely to a perfect matching. In each port path deletion of its single used port leaves two even paths, each with its unique perfect matching. Thus the external edges in a perfect matching encode precisely a permutation.

Replace every edge (including port-path edges) by its own three-edge path. An old matching edge uses both outer edges and omits the middle; an unused old edge uses its middle edge. This gives a bijection of perfect matchings before and after subdivision. Let B be the resulting bipartite graph, and H its line graph. An H-vertex is a B-edge; two H-vertices are adjacent when their B-edges meet. Independent sets of H are matchings of B. Every B-edge has an endpoint of degree two, so its line graph degree is at most 3+2−2=3. All triangles are at degree-three B-vertices, and these triangles are vertex-disjoint. The line graph is claw-free because the neighbors of a B-edge are covered by two cliques. It is perfect because a bipartite graph and each of its edge subgraphs have chromatic index equal to maximum degree (Kőnig's edge coloring theorem), which is also the clique number of the corresponding line graph when nonempty.

Let N=|E(B)| be the number of physical vertices and K=|V(B)|/2 the size of a perfect matching. Give every physical vertex absence probability q_e=t, except the middle edge in the path of old external incidence (i,j), for which q_e=t x_{ij}. All probabilities lie in [0,1] when 0<t≤1 and x is in the unit cube. Multiply the no-edge probability of H by t^{−(N−K)}. In its expansion over matchings M of B, the exact event selecting M contributes

    ∏_{e outside M}q_e · ∏_{e in M}(1−q_e).

Nonperfect matchings have higher order in t. A perfect matching contributes in the limit one monomial containing exactly the entries x_{ij} whose old external edges were selected, since those and only those marked middle edges are outside M. Every permutation has a unique extension. This proves coefficientwise equality with the permanent, with leading coefficient one, as an integer-polynomial specialization and hence under reduction to every field. The positive interpretation refers to real inputs. The count of physical vertices is Θ(m²). Replacing edges by an odd path of length 2s+1≥3 instead yields the same construction, using any even-position edge on each external path as its designated middle marker (positions start at one). This makes every cycle other than the isolated triangles arbitrarily long: in a line graph of a subcubic graph an induced cycle of length at least four traces a cycle of distinct underlying edges and vertices. The assertion about length concerns induced cycles of length ≥4; the degree-three triangles remain.

In particular the reported permanent lower bound in characteristic different from two transfers to the arithmetic border complexity of the canonical multilinear no-edge polynomial for these claw-free graphs. The exact leading identity itself is independent of that lower bound. Field scaling by t^{−(N−K)} costs a constant number of circuit nodes. Input substitutions t x_{ij} cost at most one multiplication per label. A two-stage composition of Laurent approximants, if used to derive border-to-border hardness, requires a transitivity or flattening argument; the paragraph below supplies an alternative with interpolation.

The unscaled finite graph polynomial after specialization is an ordinary polynomial in t of degree ≤N. Its coefficient at t^{N−K} is the permanent. Over an infinite field or a rational-function extension of any field, N+1 distinct t values therefore express the permanent as a fixed linear combination of N+1 ordinary specializations. For a circuit (or a coefficientwise regular Laurent approximation) for the graph polynomial, use these copies independently with constants in the extension. Interpolation of their outputs preserves regular residues. The total increase is polynomial in N, including the input preparations. Thus both exact and border superpolynomial lower bounds on the permanent imply the corresponding lower bounds on the full graph polynomial over characteristic other than two. This argument uses the released theorem over every field of that characteristic, including the rational-function extension. The identity and the separation from positive Hamiltonian approximation have no dependence on the correctness of that external lower bound. ## A Newton-polytope obstruction for general graphs

On an arbitrary graph H with N vertices, the same obstruction is controlled by extension complexity xc(STAB(H)), where STAB(H) is the convex hull of its independent-set indicators. Suppose a positive midpoint-bounded limit F of cube-preserving affine probability substitutions is nonzero. By the finite-graph monomial normal form, one may use a single monomial path q_v=t^{k_v}b_vD_v for absence probabilities (and a common positive scale B t^{−h}), with D_v nonnegative affine. Vertices fixed always present and their forced-absent neighbors can equivalently be kept as fixed states. The surviving cover events have minimum k-cost h; their complements are maximum-k-weight independent sets. This specifies a face of STAB(H) with any deterministic state pins. The normalized exact-selection expansion for the limit is a sum over its independent sets S. A vertex outside S contributes b_vD_v. A vertex in S with k_v>0 contributes 1; one in S with k_v=0 contributes 1−b_vD_v. These are all nonnegative affine functions on the cube. A factor identically zero in one state forbids that state and imposes one more coordinate-face equality. If every term were removed the limit would vanish. The deterministic state pins can be handled before the maximum-weight face, so the assertion also covers entries identically zero at the midpoint.

At the origin each nonzero local factor has initial form either a positive constant, or a homogeneous linear form with nonnegative coefficients. Among the surviving independent sets minimize the number of zero-intercept factors. This is a linear objective in the selected-vertex vector (a sum of two state-specific costs per vertex). Its minimizers form a face of the previous face. There is no cancellation in their lowest degree because each product is positive on the strict positive orthant. The lowest homogeneous part F_d is therefore a positive sum of the products of these local initial forms. Its Newton polytope has an extended formulation of size

    xc(STAB(H)) + O(N(d_loc+1)),

where d_loc is the maximum number of input coordinates in a single physical affine function. Start with an extension of the selected face, using affine equations to impose each face equality. For every vertex and each of its two states, let the state marginal be y_v or 1−y_v, with y in that face. Split each marginal among the positive labels in the relevant local initial form, one nonnegative coordinate per label and a zero-exponent label for a constant. Output the sum of all label marginals for each input coordinate. Conversely decompose y into integral states in this face and conditionally sample the local labels given each state's bit, in proportion to its split marginals. This realizes every proposed output as a convex combination of exponents that have positive coefficients in F_d. This coupling proof gives equality of polytopes. Affine face equations are free in the usual inequality count; counting them as pairs adds only O(N) after combining face equalities.

For H a line graph of a bipartite graph B, STAB(H) is the matching polytope of B. Edge nonnegativity and vertex degree bounds describe it by bipartite total unimodularity, using O(N) inequalities after deleting isolated B-vertices. From the argument in the main note, multiplicative comparison near zero with HC_m forces Newt(F_d) to be the directed tour polytope. The undirected projection and Rothvoß's lower bound give N(d_loc+1)≥2^{Ω(m)}. Since d_loc≤m², this requires exponentially many vertices, even with no locality restriction. The complete graph perfect-matching polynomial (hafnian) obeys the same lower bound. In contrast, the permanent construction above uses degree-three physical graphs, N=Θ(m²), and one-coordinate absence probabilities.

This statement is about approximation on a genuine positive neighborhood or a coefficientwise identity. Nonnegative outputs agreeing only on Boolean corners need not exhibit the same initial form.
