# A one-parameter normal form for the whole limiting polynomial

Midpoint boundedness controls values as well as support. On any fixed finite graph, a positive midpoint-bounded sequence of cube-preserving affine substitutions and positive rescalings has a subsequence converging coefficientwise. Each such limit can be reproduced on the **same graph** by a single monomial path. Each absence probability on that path is either zero or has the form t^{k_v} b_v d_v(x), with k_v a nonnegative integer, b_v>0, and d_v a nonnegative affine form with d_v(midpoint)=1. The rescaling is B t^{−h} with B>0. The input locality is preserved. If the original graph is bipartite, all k_v can be taken at most deg(v)+1 and h at most the edge count. The statement concerns the whole polynomial, including its non-Boolean values. When only corner convergence was specified, the common corner values agree with the reproduced polynomial; its interior may depend on the subsequence.

## Extracting a common polynomial

Fix a graph and take one subsequence throughout. Write a_v for the midpoint absence probability, d_{v,j}=q_{v,j}/a_v for its normalized affine form. Split the vertices into those with identically zero a_v at every retained stage and those with always positive a_v. A vertex in the former set is always present throughout the cube. If two such vertices are adjacent, the graph probability is zero. Otherwise all their neighbors must be absent. They and their neighbors can be removed in the probability computation by extracting the product of the neighbors' absence probabilities. Equivalently, in the cover-event expansion below simply ignore covers containing an always-present vertex. Every surviving cover contains all of its neighbors. Keep these classes fixed on a subsequence.

Every rescaled cover event is bounded at the midpoint. Take limits w_C of all such event weights and all normalized forms d_v. Each latter form is bounded by two throughout the cube, so its affine coefficients converge on a subsequence. Inclusion-exclusion on the finite union of the minimal cover-absent events expresses Q as a fixed signed integer linear combination of cover monomials, since intersections correspond to cover unions. Consequently the complete rescaled polynomial converges coefficientwise along this subsequence to the same linear combination of w_C times the products of d_v over C. Every subsequently reproduced weight and form therefore reproduces the entire polynomial. If no weight limit is positive, the polynomial is zero. Realize zero in that case by keeping any fixed cube parameters and scaling by t; here the convention allows a vanishing scale (i.e. h=−1). If the graph has an edge, forcing its endpoints present also realizes zero with unit scale. For nonzero limits all subsequent pole orders are nonnegative.

## Making all rates integral simultaneously

Restrict to cover events that avoid the always-present vertices. Introduce a logarithmic coordinate for c_j and each strictly positive a_v(j). After extraction each such a_v converges to A_v in [0,1]. Call it regular when A_v>0 and rare when A_v=0. Build a real matrix M with one row e_v for each regular vertex and one row (1,1_C) for each cover with w_C>0. Applied to the logarithm vector, these rows converge to log A_v and log w_C, respectively. The range of M is closed in finite dimension, so there exists z with exactly these row values. Its regular entries are the specified log A_v.

For every rare coordinate and every surviving cover of zero limiting weight, the respective row e_v or (1,1_C) tends to minus infinity on the original logarithm vector. Project that vector onto ker M. The discarded component is bounded because M is injective on the orthogonal complement of its kernel and the row values are bounded. At one sufficiently late stage, every required divergent row is therefore strictly negative on this projected vector. The kernel is rational; a rational approximation followed by a sign change and clearing denominators yields an integer vector (−h,k) in ker M with every one of these rows strictly positive. Thus k_v=0 exactly on regular vertices, k_v≥1 on rare vertices; positive covers have cost h and the other surviving covers cost strictly greater than h.

Take B=exp(z_0) and b_v=exp(z_v). For sufficiently small t>0 every q_v(t,x)=b_v t^{k_v}d_v(x) lies in [0,1]. For regular vertices this follows from q_{v,j}→A_v d_v on the cube. For rare vertices it follows from d_v≤2. Always-present vertices keep q_v=0. Replacing the rescaling by B t^{−h} gives precisely the original limits for all cover-event monomials. A rational nullspace witness can also be chosen with polynomial bit length in the number of vertices: the equalities and strict inequalities have entries in {0,1}; replace the strict right-hand sides by one after scaling, find a basic feasible point after splitting variables into nonnegative parts, and use Hadamard's determinant bound. Its integer magnitudes may grow exponentially. The next argument removes that growth for bipartite graphs.

## Low order on a bipartite graph

First ignore always-present vertices and their forced-absent neighbors. On the other vertices, the good covers are exactly the minimum-cost covers at the nonnegative costs k. A fixed forced-absent neighbor contributes the same k_v to every surviving cover, so its exponent can be reset to 1 when rare and 0 when regular. It enters the common pole order with the reset value. Its amplitude b_v remains as obtained above.

Apply the weighted bipartite vertex-cover linear program on the residual graph. By total unimodularity an integral optimum exists, and by strong duality there are nonnegative edge loads y_e with incident sum at most k_v and total load equal to minimum cover cost. Define slack s_v=k_v−Σ_{e incident to v} y_e. For every cover C the cost above optimum equals

    Σ_{v in C} s_v + Σ_{edges with both endpoints in C} y_e.

So the optimum face among covers only depends on which slacks and loads are positive. Replace each positive slack and each positive load by 1, and set k'_v to the new slack plus its incident loads. This preserves every optimal cover and all zero-cost coordinates. Every positive k_v remains positive. The new optimum equals the number of positive-load edges because a formerly optimal cover still attains that dual value. The new weights therefore give exactly the same pattern of constant weights and disappearing weights in the cover expansion. Keep B, b_v and d_v as before. All positive cover weights still have precisely their specified amplitudes, and the same polynomial limit follows.

The resulting k'_v is at most deg(v)+1. The new pole order is at most the residual edge count plus the number of rare forced-absent neighbors. Each such neighbor has a distinct incident edge outside the residual graph, proving h≤|E|. Zero-probability parameters can be kept identically zero. For a star on d leaves, positive equal-rate contributions from both its singleton-center cover and its all-leaves cover require k_center=Σ k_leaf. If every leaf is rare, k_center≥d. This example shows the degree dependence is necessary when reproducing the individual event weights on the same graph. It makes no optimality claim among all alternative representations of the resulting polynomial.

## Exponential orders can be necessary on general graphs

The linear bound for a bipartite graph has no polynomial counterpart for all graphs when retaining the coordinates of the limiting forms. The examples use repeated substitution of a five-cycle for a vertex. This is the classical stable-set substitution operation. Here its cost is an approximation exponent.

Start with a five-cycle and assign every vertex weight one. At each stage choose a vertex of weight one from the newest cycle, substitute a fresh five-cycle for it, and join each of the five new vertices to every former neighbor of the replaced vertex. Double every surviving old weight and assign weight one to the five fresh vertices. After r substitutions there are N=5+4r vertices. A vertex of the first cycle that was never chosen has weight 2^r.

The original weight vector is determined up to scale by the requirement that all its maximum-weight independent sets have equal weight. For the initial five-cycle its five nonadjacent pairs have equal total only if all five vertex weights are equal. At a substitution, an old optimal independent set avoiding the replaced vertex is unchanged, and one containing that vertex replaces it by any maximum pair on the new five-cycle. Conversely these are all the new optima: a choice entering the module forbids exactly the old neighbors, and can maximize its contribution by choosing two of the five new vertices. Every vertex occurs in an optimal independent set by induction. Comparing the five pairs while holding one exterior choice fixed forces the candidate new weights to be a common value u. Contract the fresh cycle to a single vertex of weight 2u. Equality on all old optima forces the contracted vector to be proportional to the previous vector. This recovers exactly the specified ray, including over the reals.

Let k be these positive integer weights, h the minimum cover cost, and set q_v(t,x)=t^{k_v}x_v with one independent cube coordinate per labeled vertex. The limit under scale t^{−h} is precisely the sum of squarefree monomials x_C over the minimum-weight covers C. This follows directly by summing the probabilities of the exact absence sets that cover the graph. Each optimum cover is inclusion-minimal since the weights are positive. In Q written in the independent q_v, the coefficient of any inclusion-minimal cover monomial is one, by the union-of-events identity.

Suppose a second monomial path on the same graph, with the same individual coordinate forms q'_v(t,x)=b_v t^{ell_v} x_v and all b_v>0, realizes the same polynomial with a common scalar monomial. The coefficients of the optimal cover monomials force all their new costs to be equal. Taking complements makes the sum of the new weights constant on all the original optimal independent sets, so the uniqueness argument makes ell proportional to k. The proportionality factor cannot be zero. Indeed if every ell vanished, the pole order would be zero as well. Choose an inclusion-minimal set U that is the union of at least two distinct inclusion-minimal covers. The monomial q_U has nonzero coefficient in Q: every proper subset of U contains at most one of those minimal covers if it contains any two with union in U, by minimality of U; equivalently any two distinct minimal covers contained in U have union exactly U. With j≥2 such covers, inclusion-exclusion gives coefficient 1−j. (Other minimal covers outside U do not contribute.) This coefficient stays nonzero under a nonzero scalar and positive constant coordinate scalings, but it vanishes in the claimed limit, where every monomial is an inclusion-minimal cover. The graphs have at least two distinct minimal covers throughout.

All ell_v being integers, a fresh vertex of weight one now implies the scale factor is a positive integer. Thus max ell_v≥2^r=2^{(N−5)/4}. This separates the integral orders needed to reproduce the entire fixed-coordinate polynomial on arbitrary graphs from the O(max degree) bound for all bipartite graphs. Representations that change the graph or replace one labeled coordinate by an unrelated affine form are outside this lower bound.

## Small facet coefficients suffice, and interpolation removes the parameter

More generally, suppose the stable-set polytope of the residual N-vertex graph is described by x≥0 and valid inequalities a·x≤b with nonnegative integer entries a_v≤D. On a rank-perfect graph one may take D=1. This includes perfect graphs (clique inequalities) and line graphs (the matching polytope). Restricting an inequality description to an induced subgraph amounts to setting the deleted coordinates to zero and preserves its coefficient bound. [Chvátal](https://doi.org/10.1016/0095-8956(75)90041-6) [Wagler on rank-perfect graphs](https://opendata.uni-halle.de/bitstream/1981185920/10787/1/annwagler.pdf)

For any nonnegative weight k, take an optimal LP dual of maximum-weight stable set. The nonnegative dual combination of the rows a dominates k coordinatewise. Expand the available row family by all rows obtained by zeroing any subset of coefficients, keeping each right-hand side b. All remain valid. Where the dual combination exceeds k_i, split positive dual terms as necessary into one copy of a and one copy with coordinate i zeroed, until the dual combination agrees with k_i. This changes no other coordinate or objective value. The exact-covering dual min Σ b_i y_i subject to Σ y_i a_i=k, y≥0, therefore has an optimum equal to the original support value. It has a basic optimum using at most N nonzero rows. Every maximum-weight stable set makes all these rows tight, and every independent set satisfying all of them maximizes the original weight. Give each of these rows unit multiplier. The new objective vector k' is their sum, has exactly the original zero coordinates and satisfies k'_v≤ND. Its maximum independent sets, hence its minimum covers, are unchanged. As in the bipartite proof, every previously good event keeps its precise amplitude. Restoring forced neighbors costs at most one apiece. This gives O(ND) degree per vertex and O(N²D) total degree for the entire monomial path.

In particular, on a bipartite graph the unscaled substituted probability is a polynomial in t of degree at most Σ_v k_v≤2|E|+N. Its coefficient at pole order h is the full limit divided by the constant scaling B. Evaluate at 2|E|+N+1 distinct sufficiently small positive real parameters. The coefficient is a fixed real linear combination of those evaluated probabilities by univariate interpolation. Every evaluation is an ordinary cube-preserving affine substitution on the same graph. Thus the exact circuit cost of this positive border is bounded by O((1+|E|+N)(s+1)), where s is a common upper bound on the circuit cost of each required ordinary specialization (including input-affine preparation). With coefficient bound D above there is the analogous O((1+N²D)(s+1)) conclusion on the corresponding graphs. Interpolation uses signed output combinations. This is a comparison within the specified positive substitutions and graphs; no degree claim is made for unrestricted algebraic degeneration.
