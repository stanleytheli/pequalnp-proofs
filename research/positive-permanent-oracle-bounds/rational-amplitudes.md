# Exact amplitudes for a limiting weighted permanent

Let \(m_{se}\) be a finite matrix of nonnegative integer exponents. Suppose a net of common scalars \(c_t\) and entry weights \(a_{te}\) satisfies
\[
 c_t\prod_e a_{te}^{m_{se}}\longrightarrow p_s>0
\]
for every retained outcome \(s\). Then there are fixed positive real numbers \(b,d_e\) such that \(p_s=b\prod_e d_e^{m_{se}}\) for every retained outcome. Even the approximating entries can have zeros or varying signs.

For a fixed outcome the product is eventually nonzero. Its logarithm of absolute value then equals \(\log|c_t|+\sum_e m_{se}\log|a_{te}|\). Assign zero to \(\log|0|\) at intermediate entries. A zero with a positive exponent cannot occur in this outcome eventually, and an exponent zero contributes nothing. The vector \((\log p_s)_s\) is the limit of vectors in the range of the real linear map taking \((u,v)\) to \((u+\sum_e m_{se}v_e)_s\). That range is closed in finite-dimensional space. Choose a preimage of the limit and exponentiate its coordinates.

For the permanent, outcomes are permutations and exponents are their zero-one incidence vectors. If the positive limiting permutations comprise all perfect matchings on the union of their edges, apply the theorem on that set. Set every other matrix entry to zero. The resulting nonnegative matrix realizes every permutation amplitude with one common positive multiplier. If normalized child polynomials converge pointwise, products and the finite sum also converge, giving the exact weighted permanent of the limiting children.

The Lean declarations are `PositivePermanent.positive_amplitudes` in `ToricAmplitudes.lean`, and `PositivePermanent.matching_amplitudes` and `PositivePermanent.normalized_permanent_limit` in `PermanentAmplitudes.lean`. The latter two take the positive-permutation face implication as a hypothesis. The companion `PositivePermanent.limit_face` proves it when at least one limiting amplitude is positive. These two files import only Mathlib and one another.

## Rational surviving amplitudes

For a permanent with rational positive limiting weights on the surviving matchings, the realizing matrix can also be taken rational. This refinement is a mathematical argument. Consider the lattice \(L\) in \(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z^E\) generated by \((1,\chi_M)\) over the surviving matchings, where \(E\) is their union. This lattice is saturated. Any integral \((k,z)\) in its real span has all row and column sums equal to \(k\). Add sufficiently many copies of the sum of the generators to make all entries of \(z\) nonnegative and the total degree nonnegative. The resulting integer regular bipartite multigraph decomposes into perfect matchings. Every such matching on \(E\) survives by the face property, so the shifted point and hence the original point belong to \(L\). For order zero the unique empty matching gives the statement directly.

The real amplitude theorem shows that sending a generator to its rational limiting weight defines a well-defined group homomorphism from \(L\) to the positive rational numbers under multiplication. Every signed integer relation between generators has product one because the positive real representation exists. A saturated subgroup of a finitely generated free abelian group is a direct summand. Extend the homomorphism by taking value one on a complementary summand. The images of the coordinate unit vectors give the desired rational multiplier and rational edge weights. The hypothesis concerns the individual surviving matching weights. Rationality of a sum representing the final polynomial alone does not give this local hypothesis.
