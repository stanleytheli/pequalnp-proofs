# An almost-linear upper bound for every Desarguesian projective plane

For every prime power q=pᵃ, all n=q²+q+1 projective line ORs over F_q have a bounded-fanin Boolean circuit of O(n log³ n) gates with at most ⌈log₂(n+1)⌉ NOT gates. Alongside the size/negation lower bound for all projective planes, this shows that Θ(log n) negations are necessary and sufficient for n·polylog(n) size on the entire Desarguesian family. The Walsh construction for powers of two saves one logarithm. No assertion about fast transforms for non-Desarguesian planes is needed.

For odd p, use the basis of F_q as an a-dimensional F_p-vector space. Put M=q·2^⌈log₂(2q)⌉ and choose a prime ℓ congruent to 1 modulo M. Linnik's theorem allows ℓ≤O(M^C) for an absolute constant C. Hence ℓ>q and word length log₂ ℓ=O(log q). The prime, roots of unity, slope-coordinate permutations and transform constants are fixed for each q. If a uniform description is wanted, enumerating candidates and field elements costs polynomial time in q. [Xylouris on Linnik's theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.2749)

Choose a primitive pth root ω in F_ℓ. For affine point bits f(x,y), compute the two-dimensional finite Fourier transform using the characters ω^{u·x+v·y}. For slope α, let M_α denote multiplication by α in the chosen basis. If g_α(z)=Σₓ f(x,αx+z), changing variables gives ĝ_α(v)=f̂(−M_αᵀv,v). For vertical lines the required frequencies are f̂(u,0). Extract each slice and apply the inverse transform on F_pᵃ. Since each true count is at most q<ℓ, its residue determines exactly whether the line has an occupied affine point. Add the points at infinity with OR gates.

A transform on F_pᵃ applies p-point transforms along each coordinate. Bluestein's chirp identity converts a p-point transform into one cyclic convolution of length the least power of two at least 2p−1. The necessary pth and power-of-two roots exist because their orders divide ℓ−1. For odd p one can take exponents modulo p and use the inverse of 2 in jk=(j²+k²−(j−k)²)/2. A radix-two number-theoretic transform and its inverse compute the convolution with O(p log p) field operations. The convolution kernel is fixed. The complete plane transform and the q+1 inverse slices together use O(q² a log p)=O(q² log q) operations. [Bluestein](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAU.1970.1162132)

Modular addition and subtraction use O(log q) fan-in-two Boolean gates. Multiplication by a fixed constant can be performed by modular doublings and additions in O(log²q) gates. All products with variable data in the Fourier algorithm are multiplications by fixed constants. This proves the displayed bound before limiting NOTs. The sorting-network input inverter and dual-rail evaluation in the other note preserve the asymptotic size and use at most ⌈log₂(n+1)⌉ NOTs in total. The restriction is on actual negation gates throughout the complete circuit, with arbitrary fanout.
