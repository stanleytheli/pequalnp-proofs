# Balanced tensor cuts in the same arithmetic-hard family

For a graph H on N vertices let Q_H be the multilinear polynomial for the event that the selected set of vertices contains no edge. Over the Boolean cube its truth tensor has one index of dimension two for each vertex. For a division of the vertices into two parties, flatten this tensor to a 2^{|A|} by 2^{|B|} matrix. This is the matrix operation used in the flat branch of the published tensor argument. Here an induced matching supplies an explicit invertible minor, so the calculation is independent of which branch applies to a given field. [Anthropic manuscript](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/tree/11b6c7e3c927df2658f513b6b68ea7f8929c62c5/vp-vnp)

## Making the weighted construction expand

Start with the already degree-split Hamiltonian-cycle seed. Protect its designated data leaves. Pad its remaining r vertices to k² with zero-activity vertices, for k=ceil(sqrt(r)). On those k² vertices superimpose the simple graph underlying the eight-regular Gabber–Galil graph on (ℤ/kℤ)². For each of its distinct nonloop edges ab insert a fresh helper of activity zero joined to a and b. Every helper is always absent in the weighted sum and imposes no constraint on a and b. Each padding vertex has total independent-set weight 1. Thus this superposition preserves the old partition function exactly. The final *unsigned* graph keeps the helper vertices and their edges, so they can constrain arbitrary Boolean assignments. [Gabber–Galil, 1981](https://core.ac.uk/download/pdf/82074011.pdf) [Jimbo–Maruoka, 1987](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02579322)

The generator uses the four affine neighbors (x+2y,y), (x+2y+1,y), (x,y+2x), (x,y+2x+1), together with their reverses. The standard spectral gap bound gives constant edge expansion in the eight-regular multigraph. Removing loops changes no cut, and replacing parallel edges by one reduces a cut by at most the maximum multiplicity, which is at most eight. The resulting simple graph still has a uniform positive expansion constant. Every old vertex has degree at most 3+8=11. Split degrees again with the signed equality paths. This changes the weighted partition by one known sign and produces a subcubic graph K. The designated data leaves retain degree one. Every component of K is connected to the expander, and K contains a cycle.

Replace each edge of K by a path of length L=10(6s+1), s≥0. This is implemented algebraically by first replacing each edge with a path with 6s new internal vertices of activity −1, followed by the explicit ten-edge normalized substitutions. The first replacement has the original hard edge signature, since the corresponding two-by-two transfer matrix to the sixth power is the identity. The resulting physical graph H is bipartite and subcubic. It has girth at least 3L and N=Θ(k²L)=Θ(m²L) with the quadratic-size seed. It retains both the previously proved affine Hamiltonian-cycle specialization and the explicitly optimal monotone read-two formula for the Boolean edge predicate.

## Every balanced vertex division cuts many induced edges

The vertices of K have a partition into k² connected clusters of uniformly bounded size. Start a cluster at each padded root. Place each protected data leaf with its unique owner, and place each zero helper with either of its two roots. When splitting a vertex, keep its copies and equality spacers in the cluster of that vertex. The original bounded degree gives a universal bound on cluster size. Each old expander edge has a physical edge joining its two clusters. These representatives are distinct.

After subdividing, assign the interior vertices on each within-cluster edge to that cluster. On an edge joining clusters assign the whole interior to either endpoint cluster. The clusters remain connected, each has at most bL vertices for a common b, and the quotient still contains the full simple expander as a subgraph. There are at least aLk² vertices for a fixed a>0, since the expander has linearly many edges.

Consider a cut of H with at least N/3 vertices on each side. If at least δk² clusters meet both sides, their internal connectivity supplies δk² distinct cut edges. Otherwise each side has at least (a/(3b)−δ)k² clusters lying wholly in it. In the expander, the set of wholly left clusters has at least h(a/(3b)−δ)k² edges leaving it, where h>0 is an expansion constant and both it and its complement have this many vertices. At most 8δk² of those edges have a mixed cluster as the other endpoint. Choosing δ>0 small enough leaves Ω(k²) edges between wholly opposite clusters. Their distinct representative physical edges cross the cut. This proves an Ω(k²) bound for every balanced cut, uniformly in the chosen L.

In a graph of maximum degree Δ one can greedily retain at least 1/(2Δ²) of any set of crossing edges as an induced matching. After retaining an edge uv discard all candidate edges incident to either endpoint or any neighbor of either endpoint. Since adjacent u and v belong to each other's neighborhoods, at most 2Δ vertices can be involved, and at most 2Δ² edges are discarded per step. Thus every balanced cut of H crosses an induced matching of size Ω(k²).

## Rank and optimal bond dimension

Fix every vertex outside the matching endpoints to Boolean zero. On the remaining endpoints only the matching edges survive. The minor of the independent-set tensor is E to the k-th tensor power, now writing k for the matching size, where E=[[1,1],[1,0]]. Its determinant in dimension two is −1, and its inverse [[0,1],[1,−1]] exists in every field. Thus the minor has rank 2^k in every characteristic. The Boolean edge-detection tensor 1−Q_H has rank at least 2^k−1 on the same cut. Passing from the truth table to the coefficients of the multilinear extension acts by an invertible two-by-two change of basis on each variable, so the same flattening-rank bounds hold for coefficient tensors.

Every unrooted binary tree with N leaves has an edge separating at least N/3 leaves on each side. A tree tensor network factorizes the flattening across such an edge through its bond index. The bond dimension is therefore at least 2^{Ω(k²)}, where k² once again denotes the number of root clusters. Vanishing minors make this a border lower bound as well on each fixed tree. There are finitely many binary leaf trees at each N.

There is also an upper bound 2^{O(k²)} independent of path length. In a path decomposition keep all original vertices of K, then traverse each replacement path in sequence, keeping two adjacent internal vertices at a time. This gives width O(k²). Equivalently, a left-to-right contraction can first record all original vertex bits and then remember the preceding internal bit on the current path. Its transfer matrices have at most 2^{O(k²)} states and handle arbitrary values of the physical inputs. Hence the minimum possible maximum bond dimension, optimizing over all binary tensor trees, is 2^{Θ(k²)}=2^{Θ(N/L)} for both Boolean-value and coefficient tensors. A constant-size extra block at the output implements complementation.

For L=10 this is exponential in N, while general arithmetic circuits have the weaker superpolynomial lower bound inherited from the published Hamiltonian-cycle theorem. Growing L polynomially in m gives girth at least N^α for any fixed α<1 without losing the superpolynomial arithmetic lower bound. The rank statement is a direct tensor calculation. Prior graph knowledge-compilation lower bounds already use expanders and matching restrictions. The combined construction places these properties on the same explicitly specialized, read-two family. [Bova, Capelli, Mengel and Slivovsky](https://www.lix.polytechnique.fr/~mengel/papers/cnf-to-dnnf-lower-bound.pdf)
