# Approximate tensor-tree width for all symmetric statistics of path violations

Start with the bounded-degree expander-overlay core of the Hamiltonian-cycle construction, or with any bounded-degree expander, and replace each of its g edges by a private path of length at least ten. The lengths can differ arbitrarily. At a Boolean vertex assignment, the flag bₑ is one if path e contains an occupied edge. Choose any nonconstant symmetric Boolean function F on the g flags. Its truth tensor has one binary mode per **physical vertex**. Write Dε for the least possible maximum bond of a real binary tensor tree approximating this tensor in uniform absolute error ε. The same definition on [0,1]ᴺ uses the canonical multilinear extension of the Boolean function and the usual Bernoulli evaluation at each leaf.

Let δₑ(F) denote the minimum total degree of a real polynomial approximating F with uniform error e on {0,1}ᵍ. For all 0<ε≤1/3,

    log₂(2Dε) = Θ(δ_ε(F)).

The constants are independent of F, ε, g and the subdivision lengths, for a fixed expander family and degree bound. If s is the maximum of min(j,g−1−j) over transitions F(j)≠F(j+1), where F(j) denotes the value at weight j, then this common order is

    min{ g, √(g(s+1)) + √(g log(1/ε)) }.

In particular, OR has logarithmic width Θ(min{g,√(g log(1/ε))}), while a threshold with a transition near the middle already requires logarithmic width Θ(g) at constant error. The logarithm here is of bond dimension, so either claim gives an exponential width in its displayed exponent. The OR approximant can also preserve its zero exactly, yielding the same tradeoff for relative error in the probability of an edge. Relative error for the complementary probability of no edge requires full exponential width, even at constant accuracy, by the triangular-minor argument.

## Upper bound from a polynomial with controlled coefficients

Write w=Σbᵢ. Symmetrizing an ε/4-approximating multilinear polynomial over all permutations of its variables gives q(w), a univariate polynomial of degree d≤g, with |q(w)|≤2 at integer weights 0,…,g. This follows by averaging a degree-j monomial to binom(w,j)/binom(g,j). The sharp symmetric approximate-degree theorem gives d=O(δ_ε(F)); if ε lies below 2⁻ᵍ use the exact polynomial and the degree lower bound at 2⁻ᵍ. [Paturi](https://doi.org/10.1145/129712.129758) [de Wolf, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0802.1816)

Here is an elementary coefficient bound which avoids a logarithmic loss. For d≥1 let k=⌊g/d⌋≥g/(2d). Interpolate q in the variable z=w/g from its d+1 values at zⱼ=jk/g. In the Boolean variables, each factor z−zᵢ has coefficient mass at most 2 because z has nonnegative coefficients summing to one and 0≤zᵢ≤1. The jth Lagrange denominator has magnitude (k/g)ᵈ j!(d−j)!. Consequently q(Σbᵢ) has coefficient mass at most

    2·2ᵈ (g/k)ᵈ Σⱼ 1/[j!(d−j)!] ≤ 2(8e)ᵈ.

Reducing bᵢ²=bᵢ cannot increase this bound. For g bounded the constants absorb the few small instances. Sample signed monomials by coefficient mass as in the companion approximation proof. The resulting polynomial has at most O((8e)²ᵈ·g/ε²) terms, each of degree at most d, and uniformly approximates F to within ε. When ε≥2⁻ᵍ, log(g)+log(1/ε)=O(d) by de Wolf's lower bound. For smaller ε an exact truth tensor has bond at most 2^O(g) by retaining all root bits and a count of how many paths have fired. Thus in either case one common path order, storing the ≤2d endpoints of each sampled monomial, gives a tensor train of bond 2^O(δ_ε(F)). For OR subtract q(0) before sampling. This costs at most another ε/4 in the error, removes the constant coefficient, and makes the approximation zero whenever all flags vanish. Bernoulli interpolation on physical vertex inputs carries the absolute bound to [0,1]ᴺ and the relative bound to the multilinear OR.

## Two local tables on every divided path

Balance the tree cut by the original expander vertices. For the overlay take a designated physical root as an anchor in each of its constant-size connected core clusters. Give weight one to the anchor leaves and zero to all other leaves. A centroid of this weighted degree-three tree has an incident edge separating between one third and two thirds of the anchors, for large orders. Expander edges between opposite anchor sets number Ω(g). For each of these expander edges, join the two anchors in the core through their clusters and the representative edge between clusters. Each core edge lies in only constantly many such walks: a walk stays in the two incident clusters, and the quotient expander has bounded degree. The anchors have opposite colors, so each walk contains at least one subdivided core edge whose endpoint colors differ. Bounded congestion therefore supplies Ω(g) **distinct core paths** divided by this tree cut. This proof uses no bound on the ratio of path lengths. For a core which is itself an expander, the two anchors are simply the endpoints of a crossing expander edge. From these split paths retain a constant fraction with pairwise-disjoint core endpoints by a greedy matching in the bounded-degree core. Thus any tree's balanced cut has at least 4t such paths, where t=Θ(g) and t≤g/8.

On one selected path choose adjacent vertices on opposite sides of the cut and give them the two parties' bits x,y. All other vertices on the path are zero. Its flag is xy. The same path can implement x OR y. Choose opposite-side vertices at distance at least half of this path’s length. If the two endpoints have the same color, use an interior vertex of the other color and the farther endpoint. Pin one of the adjacent vertices beside each chosen vertex to one, choosing interior neighbors where an endpoint is involved. With path length at least ten the two small edges are separated and do not interact. Using an interior neighbor is always possible, even when the chosen vertex is next to an endpoint. All other vertices on the path are zero. Its flag is then exactly x OR y. A path outside this chosen family can be forced to zero or one independently, even when its endpoints belong to selected paths. Set the first and last interior vertices to zero and, for a one, insert two adjacent ones in the middle. These choices determine the complete physical assignment without any contradiction at a shared vertex.

## Transfer the hardest transition of F

Reflect the weight if necessary to place a transition at its distance s from the nearer end. Choose B=max(0,s−⌊t/2⌋), well within g−4t, and write H(j)=F(B+j) in the unreflected case or F(g−B−j) in the reflected case. Its chosen transition is at min(s,⌊t/2⌋), so Paturi's bound gives constant-error approximate degree Ω(√(g(s+1))) on these t bits, since t/g has a positive constant lower bound. At vanishing error de Wolf's characterization gives the additional Ω(√(g log(1/ε))) term until saturation.

Use four selected paths per pattern-matrix coordinate. In the AND version Alice supplies the local bit string (z₁,1−z₁,z₂,1−z₂) and Bob supplies a one-hot index choosing one of these four entries. Their total number of positive flags in each block is the selected bit, optionally flipped. Force B unselected paths to one and the rest to zero. In the reflected version use the OR table with both parties' individual bits complemented on each path. This complements each AND flag, so the sum of its four flags is four minus the selected bit. Force B unselected paths to zero and the others to one. Thus the sign version of the (2t,t,H)-pattern matrix occurs as a restriction of the signed physical flattening. A repeated occurrence of an input bit here means selecting rows or columns tied to that bit and does not increase rank.

A real rank-D approximant of the original 0/1 matrix with error ε gives a signed approximant of rank at most D+1 and error 2ε. At fixed ε≤1/3, Sherstov's theorem at tolerance η strictly above 2ε and below 1 gives log(D+1)≥Ω(deg₁/₃ H). Fixed tolerances in (0,1) for sign outputs are equivalent up to a constant degree factor by amplification. For sufficiently small ε let η=√(2ε). The pattern theorem gives

    log(D+1) ≥ Ω(deg_η H) − O(log(1/ε)).

Apply de Wolf to H with the corresponding 0/1 error η/2. When log(1/ε)≤c g for a sufficiently small constant c, the subtractive term is absorbed by the square-root term. At still smaller ε use monotonicity from ε=exp(−c g), giving Ω(g). This proves the lower estimate at every claimed accuracy. [Sherstov, Theorem 1.4](https://doi.org/10.1137/080733644) [de Wolf, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0802.1816)

The symmetric approximation and pattern-matrix theorems are established results. This proof applies them to the entire family of symmetric path statistics on the same sparse physical graphs, optimizing over all binary tensor trees. The Boolean formula optimum and arithmetic Hamiltonian projection in the other notes concern the edge-detection member of this family.
