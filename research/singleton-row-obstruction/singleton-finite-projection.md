# A finite projection for singleton row normals

Let a seven-dimensional complex linear space in \(\mathbb C^{4\times5}\) preserve the value 120 of the rectangular permanent after translation by the all-ones matrix. Assume its projections to all pairs of rows are injective, the four single-row projections have rank four, three row normals have full support and the fourth is supported on the first column alone. The [full-row argument](full-row-obstruction.md) classifies the relative coordinates of each pair of full normals. **Theorem.** At least one of the three pairs of full normals has only two relative phase values. More concretely, its two unnormalized relative coordinate phases are opposite fourth roots of unity. The remaining two-group cases and arbitrary seven-planes remain open.

Write \(v_{ij}=E_{ij}x_j\) for the full normals before normalizing their coordinate sums, with \(E_{0j}=1\), \(E_{i0}=1\), and \(E_{ij}\) a fourth root of unity. All \(x_j\) and full row totals are nonzero. The singleton normal has phase one at column zero and zero elsewhere. For each full/full pair, the two possible orders of the noncentral phase groups give the corresponding homogeneous quadratic on the masses, as in the full-row proof. For a singleton/full pair the [sparse/full classification](sparse-high-pair.md) gives disjoint nonempty ordered subsets \(P,Q\subseteq\{1,2,3,4\}\), with 50 possibilities. For this pair let \(w_j=E_{ij}x_j\), \(s=x_0\), \(T=\sum_{j=1}^4 w_j\), and \(R=\sum_{j\notin\{0\}\cup P\cup Q} w_j\). Its equation reduces to
\[
      q_{i,P,Q}=\Big(\sum_{j\in P}w_j\Big)T-s(s+R)=0 .
\]
Indeed before expansion it is \((s+T)\sum_P w_j+s\sum_Qw_j-s(s+T)=0\).

Quotienting by the three full-row permutations and all permutations of the four missing columns gives 203 admissible full phase shapes. Of these, 139 have three three-ratio pairs. Each has eight possible orientations of its three quadratics. Their numbers with three, four and five distinct full phase columns are 64, 672 and 376 respectively. The verifier regenerates the shapes and orientations directly. The following reductions work simultaneously over the \(50^3\) low-pair choices for each high orientation.

## Three or four distinct phase columns

Combine all columns that have the same full phase vector into one aggregate mass. The three high quadratics depend only on these aggregates. Each singleton mass and the three full row totals must be nonzero; a repeated group's sum can vanish. The same [Macaulay contraction and multiplication argument](full-row-obstruction.md) as for the full-row theorem gives exact Gaussian integer operators \(A_g\) of dimension \(b\) for the remaining aggregate masses. A complex candidate supplies a common left eigenvector with eigenvalues proportional to those masses. The common scale is immaterial. In all 64 cases with three aggregates the required open locus is already empty.

With four aggregates, there is exactly one double column. Use \(A_g+tI,A_g-tI\) for its true masses, and twice each remaining aggregate operator, so that the total masses remain proportional to the recovered ones. Each finite complex split is covered. Each low equation gives a matrix polynomial \(q(X(t))\) of degree at most two. Its Gaussian determinant has degree at most \(2b\). All five weights and three full totals must be nonzero at an actual point.

## Five distinct phase columns

The three high quadrics now describe a projective curve before imposing low equations. Choose a nonzero coordinate as affine anchor \(u\); physical weights require every coordinate to be nonzero. An integral linear substitution makes the five homogeneous coordinates linear forms in \(y_1,y_2,y_3,u,t\), with invertible transformation and the anchor equal to \(u\). The verifier tests pivots of the three leading quadrics in \(y\) at \(u=t=0\). For degrees two, three and four it obtains respectively three, one and zero free monomials. Together with the four monomials of degree zero or one this gives eight basis monomials with degrees \(d=(0,1,1,1,2,2,2,3)\).

For each degree, invert the selected square pivot matrix over the Gaussian rationals and check its integer adjugate identity. The resulting linear consequences express every pivot monomial, at a candidate, in terms of the free monomials and previously reduced lower-degree monomials, with powers of \(u,t\) preserving total degree. After setting \(u=1\), multiplication of each basis monomial by a physical coordinate yields an \(8\times8\) polynomial matrix \(X_j(t)\). At a candidate, the row of basis values is nonzero because it contains 1. It is a common left eigenvector of the five operators, with eigenvalues equal to the physical weights times a common nonzero Gaussian rational scalar. This construction requires only the reduction identities. It also works on a nonreduced fibre.

Entry \((a,b)\) of \(X_j\) has degree in \(t\) at most \(1+d_b-d_a\). In particular it has degree at most four. The weight matrices are regenerated at five integer points with fixed Gaussian integer denominators and recovered by Newton interpolation. A sixth integer point checks reconstruction. For a homogeneous quadratic \(q\), its operator entries have degrees at most \(2+d_b-d_a\). Along each permutation term of its determinant the degree differences telescope, so \(\deg\det q(X)\le16\).

The determinants are reconstructed over the integers by one large-integer evaluation. If the absolute real and imaginary coefficient sum in each weight entry is bounded by \(W\), its complex magnitude is at most \(W\) on \(|t|=1\). In operator dimension \(b\), each low-equation entry has magnitude at most \(21bW^2\) there, by the formula for \(q\). Each determinant coefficient has magnitude at most \(b!(21bW^2)^b\) by the permutation expansion and Cauchy's inequality. A signed radix strictly larger than twice that bound recovers both Gaussian components uniquely from the integer evaluation. This also applies to the four-group determinants. The rational polynomial \((\Re D)^2+(\Im D)^2=D\overline D\), where conjugation is on coefficients only, preserves every complex root of the Gaussian determinant.

## Removing generic forbidden directions

Some determinants vanish identically because the operators also represent points at which a required weight or row total is zero. Choose a product \(H\) of operators for the required nonzero forms. At a genuine candidate any common evaluation vector \(e\) satisfies \(eH=h e\) for a nonzero scalar \(h\), so \(e\) belongs to the row space of \(H\). If its low equation \(q=0\) also holds, then \(e q(X)=0\), and right multiplication by \(q(X)\) loses rank on that row space. Consequently
\[
       \operatorname{rank}(Hq(X))<\operatorname{rank}H
       \le r_{\rm gen}.
\]
This remains true when the rank of \(H\) drops at the candidate.

A finite-field pivot hint chooses the factors and a proposed generic rank \(r\). If \(h\) factors were used, every \((r+1)\)-minor of \(H\) has degree at most \(4h(r+1)\). At one more distinct integer arguments than this bound the verifier computes the rank exactly over \(\mathbb Q(\mathrm i)\) by fraction-free elimination. Rank at most \(r\) on all those fibres proves the generic upper bound. Thus each \(r\)-minor of \(Hq(X)\) is a necessary polynomial condition. Multiple minors can be combined by exact gcd. The coefficient bound follows from multiplying the original entry bounds, giving an entry bound \((5bW)^h b(21bW^2)\), followed by the same determinant and Cauchy estimates. Finite-field calculations select minors; the norm coefficients themselves are reconstructed over the Gaussian integers.

## Optionwise intersections

In six four-group orientations the total unions are too large because some individual low-row determinants vanish identically. For each ordered triple of choices take the primitive gcd of its three rational norm polynomials, treating the zero polynomial as imposing no condition. A constant gcd excludes the triple. All other triples must also pass the necessary rank-seven phase-only projection test. The verifier checks each one that passes directly against the full set of six homogeneous weight conics using the exact [Macaulay contraction and finite Gram computations](sparse-row-obstruction.md). This includes triples in which all three option norms are zero. Four orientations have zero surviving triples. The other two exclude 594 triples by an empty open locus and 56 by a nonzero Gram minor.

## Finite fibres and the Gram test

For every full row take the union of zeros of the nonzero norm polynomials for its 50 choices, or the entire parameter line if some choice has zero norm. Intersect the three unions by exact primitive gcd and lcm. Finer factors follow from gcd with individual norm polynomials. For each nonconstant remaining factor \(f(t)\), substitute its rational companion matrix for \(t\), using tensor products with the basis operators. Eigenvectors of the companion at every root of \(f\) preserve the common left-evaluation property. Multiplying by a common nonzero denominator keeps every weight proportional to the original one.

Successively restrict the row representation to the image of each nonzero required form. An actual evaluation vector lies in that row image by the preceding eigenvector argument. Exact Gaussian row coordinates and invariance under each weight operator are checked by adjugate identities before changing the representation. Reduction modulo a split prime can now show that the concatenated columns of some selected low equations span the representation. An integer minor nonzero modulo that prime is nonzero over the Gaussian rationals. The three low equations could otherwise have a common left annihilator.

When necessary, impose the physical rank requirement of seven on every two-row projection, and the [rank-at-most-six Gram obstruction](full-row-obstruction.md) on the eleven physical covectors. On a finite fibre, a genuine point has a left evaluation vector \(e\) annihilating its three low equations. For any seven selected combinations of Gram rows, their evaluations across all eleven columns admit a nontrivial linear dependence. The resulting tensor of that dependence with \(e\) is a nonzero left annihilator of the concatenation of the projected Gram with seven copies of the three low equations. Thus full row rank modulo a split prime excludes the candidate. The verifier computes this augmented rank efficiently by first finding the modular left kernel of the three low equations and checking the Gram rows on that kernel. This modular test proves that a full-size minor of the original augmented matrix is nonzero in characteristic zero, independently of any possible rank change of an intermediate kernel.

The program uses only exact identities or split-prime certificates of nonvanishing rank. A failed search for a pivot or for a degree bound never certifies an orientation.

All 64 three-group, 672 four-group, and 376 five-group high orientations are excluded. Since the high-pair classification permits only two and three relative phase values, this proves the theorem.
