# Three distinct full phase columns beside a singleton row normal

Let \(L\subset\mathbb C^{4\times5}\) have dimension seven, preserve the value 120 of the rectangular permanent after translation by the all-ones matrix, and project injectively onto every pair of rows. Assume all row projections have dimension four, with three full-support normals and one normal supported on a single coordinate. Use the fourth-root phase normalization and row-pair classification in the [full-row argument](full-row-obstruction.md). In that notation each normalized full normal has entries \(E_{ij}x_j/s_i\), where \(s_i=\sum_jE_{ij}x_j\), all \(x_j,s_i\) are nonzero, and the first full phase row consists of ones. Call a full pair three-ratio when its relative phase takes three distinct values and two-ratio when it takes two.

**Theorem.** If there are exactly three distinct columns in the three-row full phase matrix, all three pairs of full normals are two-ratio.

The exact [phase enumeration](verify_three_columns.py) gives 64 orientations with three three-ratio pairs, 40 with two, and four with none. It gives no orientation with exactly one. The 64 cases with three such pairs have empty aggregate spectra, as verified by the three high quadrics. The broader [singleton finite-projection theorem](https://github.com/stanleytheli/pequalnp-proofs/tree/f57b1176c442801266c77c5d21e3c7008b9e1d3e/research/singleton-row-obstruction) also rules out that configuration. This note handles the remaining 40 orientations with two three-ratio pairs. Their 50³ choices of sparse/full-pair orientations require no enumeration.

## Aggregate masses and two split parameters

Let \(C_1,C_2,C_3\) be the three groups of columns with the same full phase and \(a_k=\sum_{j\in C_k}x_j\). The two three-ratio pairs give homogeneous quadratics in \((a_1,a_2,a_3)\). A full row total is nonzero, a singleton aggregate must be nonzero, and the two-ratio pair requires \(s_i\pm s_k\ne0\). The [Macaulay contraction and open restriction](full-row-obstruction.md) gives an empty locus in ten orientations. In the other 30 it gives Gaussian integer matrices \(A_k\) of size \(b\) equal to one or two. A genuine evaluation supplies a common nonzero row eigenvector \(e\) of the \(A_k\) with eigenvalues proportional to the three masses. The verifier checks the pairwise commutation identities for these matrices.

In each group of size \(h\) give its columns the operator baseline \(6A_k/h\). For each column other than the first in its group, add a scalar parameter times \(I_b\) and subtract the same amount from the first column. There are exactly two parameters, \(t,u\), across the three groups. Their finite complex values realize all five masses with the required common aggregate scale. Write \(X_j(t,u)\) for these weight operators. The three full totals remain constant matrices across the splits.

## Eight physical covectors

Use the three full row normals, the unit vector on the singleton row's supported coordinate, the three full/full tangent covectors, and the total-sum covector. These eight belong to the annihilator of \(L\) at the physical weights. Write them as \(F\) over the commutative algebra generated by the \(A_k\); at a common evaluation all eight rows recover the physical covectors up to nonzero row scales. In the notation of the [full-row argument](full-row-obstruction.md), form the eight-by-eight matrix with algebra entries
\[
 G=F K F^t,\qquad K=(3I_4-\mathbf1\mathbf1^t)\otimes(4I_5-\mathbf1\mathbf1^t).
\]
The superscript here transposes only the physical covector slots. On evaluation at a genuine space, \(G\) has scalar rank at most six, so every block of its adjugate has \(e\) in its left kernel.

Each physical covector is affine in \(t,u\). In either individual parameter its derivative has entries only in the three full row slots, on one column-difference vector. This holds even for the full/full pair covectors because the full totals are constant and the two columns of the split share every full phase and its corresponding pair group. The derivative thus factors through a free module of rank three. In each seven-by-seven minor of \(G\), the Cauchy–Binet formula uses one seven-row minor from each side of \(F K F^t\). Taking more than three varying factors on either side gives zero, by their rank-three factorization. Hence every scalar entry of every adjugate block has degree at most six in \(t\) and at most six in \(u\).

For exact evaluation the program computes a fixed nonzero scalar multiple of the adjugate using Newton identities over the commutative operator algebra. Starting with \(B_0=I\), form
\[
 B_k=k G B_{k-1}-I_8\otimes\operatorname{tr}_8(G B_{k-1})\qquad(1\le k\le7).
\]
The trace sums the eight diagonal algebra blocks. This recurrence gives \(B_7=-7!\operatorname{adj}G\), with the sign from dimension eight. It uses integer arithmetic. On the seven-by-seven grid of consecutive parameter values the program obtains every coefficient by finite differences, checks exact division by the factorials, and verifies the recovered polynomials at two additional points.

## Identities on the required open locus

For each block \(B_{7,ij}\) take its individual polynomial columns, and multiply by all monomials in \(t,u\) up to a fixed total degree. Stacking their coefficients gives an exact Gaussian integer matrix. A complex candidate would have \(e\) annihilating all these columns at its two parameters.

The target is either the identity matrix, the product of the physical weight operators on the varying coordinates, or a positive power of that product. At a candidate it acts on \(e\) by a nonzero scalar, since every physical weight is nonzero. The verifier proves that every polynomial column of the target lies in the generated span. Finite-field elimination supplies only the suggested independent rows and pivot columns. It inverts the chosen pivot block by a Gaussian adjugate, multiplies back against **all** coefficient columns, and checks the resulting integer identity exactly. The nonzero pivot determinant allows division over \(\mathbb C\). Evaluation would make \(e\) annihilate the target, contrary to its nonzero eigenvalue.

The supplied degree and target choices select a small identity to check for each orientation. Their validity follows from the checked identities. Degrees four through eleven suffice; the largest chosen pivot square has size 96. These identities exclude all 30 nonempty aggregate spectra and prove the theorem together with the ten empty cases and phase enumeration.
